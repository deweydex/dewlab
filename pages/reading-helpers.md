# Reading helpers

A page here is mostly reading. If English is not your first language, if
reading takes you longer than you would like, or if you would rather listen,
these can help. Some are part of dewlab, and some come with your browser.

## On every dewlab page

**See what a term means.** Some words in the reading have a faint dotted
line under them. The course uses each of these words in a particular way.
Point at one, or tap it, and a small box shows what it means. With the
keyboard, press Tab to reach the word, and Enter to open the Reference at
it. If you would rather not see the lines, open **Settings**, then
**Reading**, and turn off **Definitions on hover**.

**Look a word up.** Select a word in the reading. If the Reference knows it,
a small **Look up** button appears. Press it, and the Reference opens at that
word. If you met it on an earlier page, it links back there.

**Change how the page looks.** Open **Settings**, at the top right, and
then **Appearance**. There you can choose:

- a bigger text size, and narrower or wider lines;
- light or dark;
- a font built for easier reading: Lexend, or OpenDyslexic;
- **High contrast**, which gives black text on white, or white on black in
  dark mode;
- **Patterns in pictures**, which adds stripes to the coloured parts of
  some pictures, so you can tell the parts apart without the colours.
  **High contrast** turns it on too;
- **Reduce motion**, which stops things moving on the page.

Your choices stay the same from page to page.

## Translating a page

Your browser can translate a page into your own language.

- **Chrome:** right-click anywhere on the page, and choose **Translate to**
  and your language.
- **Edge:** right-click the page and choose **Translate to** and your
  language, or use the translate button in the address bar.
- **Firefox:** use the translate button in the address bar. Firefox
  translates on your own computer.
- **Safari:** use the translate button in the address bar, where your
  language is offered.

Translation changes the reading, and leaves three things as they are: the
code in the cells and examples, what a cell prints, and the maths. A
translated `print` would not run, so the code stays in Python.

A machine makes the translation, so now and then a sentence comes out
strangely. If one does not make sense, the same menu can show the page in
English again.

## Listening to a page

- **Edge:** right-click the page and choose **Read aloud**. Edge reads from
  where you are, and highlights each word as it goes.
- **Chrome:** right-click the page and choose **Open in reading mode**. The
  panel that opens has a play button.
- **iPhone and iPad:** in Settings, open **Accessibility**, then **Spoken
  Content**, and turn on **Speak Selection**. Select some text, and choose
  **Speak**.
- **Android:** turn on **Select to Speak** in the Accessibility settings.
  Then tap its button, and tap the text you want to hear.

## Edge's Immersive Reader

Edge can show the reading on its own, in a clean layout with bigger spacing,
colours you choose, and a picture dictionary. Press **F9**, or the book
button in the address bar, where it appears. Immersive Reader shows the
reading only, so come back to the page to run the cells.
