# Planning a site

Five pages with no plan tend to disagree with each other: a heading style
here, a different one there, a page nobody links to. A plan written
first is what keeps five pages feeling like one site.

## Two site maps

Both of these describe the same five pages. Only one tells you anything
useful.

> Page 1, page 2, page 3, page 4, page 5.

> Home — introduces the site and links to the other four.
> About — the background a visitor needs before the rest makes sense.
> Gallery — images, linked from Home.
> Contact — a form, linked from every page's navigation.
> Resources — further links, linked from About and Home.

The second one names what each page is for and how a visitor reaches it.
That is a site map: not a list of files, but a list of jobs and
connections.

## Why this happens

A visitor never sees your plan. They see whether the site holds together.
Is the navigation the same on every page? Does a heading style mean the
same thing twice? Does the page they land on tell them where to go next?
Planning is where those decisions get made once, instead of once per
page while you are also trying to write HTML.

Three questions do most of the work.

**Who is this for?** A visitor arriving with no context, deciding in a
few seconds whether to stay.

**What is each page for?** Not its topic alone, but its job. What should
a visitor do or learn there, and where should they go next?

**How do the pages connect?** Which pages link to which, and why. A page
nothing links to might as well not exist.

A wireframe, a rough sketch of a page's layout, answers a fourth
question before any CSS exists. Where does everything sit, roughly,
before you decide exactly how it looks?

## Your turn

Let's open your fork of `project_wad` and its `planning.md`. Try
answering the purpose and audience questions, and filling in the site

Sketch a wireframe for at least one page, however rough — it does not

## What you have now

A plan a visitor never sees, and a site that holds together because of
it.

- **Site map** — what each page is for, and how the pages connect.
- **Wireframe** — a rough sketch of a page's layout, made before its CSS.
