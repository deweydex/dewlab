# Dividing fractions: how many fit? — Practice

Small problems on one idea: dividing by a fraction asks how many of it
fit. Try each problem before you open anything under it. There is no
hurry, and no score. Some problems come with numbers, shapes or
letters. Choose the way you like in the box under the title.

## 1. How many fit?

```question
id: how-many-fit-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

The number of quarters in 2 pizzas is {8|6|1/2}.

The number of halves in 5 pizzas is {10|5/2|7}.

The number of tenths in 5 pizzas is {50|1/2|15}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Picture one pizza cut into quarters. How many slices?
2. Now picture 2 of those pizzas.
3. Do the same for halves, and then for tenths.

**Think about:** the smaller the slice, the more slices fit.

**Try this next:** how many thirds are there in 4 pizzas?

</details>

## 5. The same, or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

6 ÷ 1/2 and 6 × 2 are {the same amount|different amounts}.

6 ÷ 2 and 6 × 1/2 are {the same amount|different amounts}.

6 ÷ 1/2 and 6 × 1/2 are {different amounts|the same amount}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. 6 ÷ 1/2 asks how many halves fit in 6 pizzas.
2. 6 × 1/2 is half of 6.
3. 6 ÷ 2 asks how many 2s fit in 6.

**Think about:** 2 and 1/2 are partners. Dividing by one is the same
as multiplying by the other.

**Try this next:** are 10 ÷ 5 and 10 × 1/5 the same amount?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$6 \div \frac{1}{2} = 12$ and $6 \times 2 = 12$, the same. $6 \div 2 =
3$ and $6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$, the same too. But $6 \div
\frac{1}{2} = 12$ and $6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$ are different.

</details>

## 6. Close to 0, a half, or 1?

No working needed at first. Picture the slices, and guess.

```question
id: close-to-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

1/10 ÷ 1/2 is closest to {0|1/2|1}.

1/2 ÷ 1/2 is exactly {1|1/4|0}.

1/3 ÷ 1/2 is closest to {1/2|0|1}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Turn 1/2 over, and multiply: 1/10 × 2.
2. How many halves fit in a half?
3. 1/3 × 2 is 2/3. Is that nearer to a half, or to 1?

**Think about:** 2/3 is 0.67, to two places after the point.

**Try this next:** is 1/4 ÷ 1/2 closest to 0, a half or 1?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$\frac{1}{10} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{5}$, which is 0.2, nearest
to 0. $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{2} = 1$: one half fits in a half.
$\frac{1}{3} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{3}$, which is about 0.67. That
is 0.17 from a half and 0.33 from 1, so it is closest to a half.

</details>

## 7. What went differently here?

<img src="half-in-quarters.svg" alt="A fraction wall of three bars of the same length. The top bar is one whole. The second is cut into 2 halves, and the left half is shaded. The third is cut into 4 quarters, and the 2 left quarters are shaded.">

Somebody found $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4}$ like this. They turned
over the *first* fraction, and multiplied:

$$\frac{2}{1} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

The wall shows how many quarters fit in a half. Does it agree with
$\frac{1}{2}$?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Count the shaded quarters under the shaded half.
2. Is that count 1/2?
3. Which fraction do we divide *by*? That is the one to turn over.

**Think about:** in 10 ÷ 2, the 2 is the size of each group. In
1/2 ÷ 1/4, which number is the size of each slice?

**Try this next:** find 1/2 ÷ 1/4 by turning over the other fraction.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

No. Two quarters fit in a half, so the answer is 2. The fraction to
turn over is the one we divide by, $\frac{1}{4}$:

$$\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{1} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$$

</details>

## 8. Two paths, one answer

How many two thirds fit in 2? Here are two paths.

- Path one: cut 2 pizzas into thirds, and count pairs of thirds.
- Path two: turn $\frac{2}{3}$ over, and multiply.

Do the two paths meet?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Path one: 2 pizzas in thirds make how many thirds?
2. Put them in pairs. How many pairs?
3. Path two: 2/3 turned over is 3/2. What is 2 × 3/2?

**Think about:** why the pairs matter in path one.

**Try this next:** how many three quarters fit in 3? Try both paths.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Path one: 2 pizzas make 6 thirds, and 6 thirds make 3 pairs. Path two:
$2 \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$. Both paths give 3.

</details>

## 10. Sharing half a pizza

Half a pizza is shared between 3 people. How much of a whole pizza does
each person get? This is $\frac{1}{2} \div 3$.

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. 3 is the same as 3/1. Turn it over.
2. Multiply 1/2 by that.
3. Or picture it: cut the half into 3 equal pieces.

**Think about:** sharing between 3 is the same as taking a third of it.

**Try this next:** a third of a pizza, shared between 2 people.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$\frac{1}{2} \div 3 = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$.
It is the square from *Multiplying fractions: a fraction of a
fraction* again: a third of a half is one sixth.

</details>

## 11. None of the pizza

This one goes past the page. On the page, $1 \div 0$ made Python stop
with an error. What about $0 \div \frac{1}{4}$? How many quarter slices
fit in no pizza at all? Guess first, then run the cell.

```python exec
id: none-of-the-pizza-1
from fractions import Fraction

print(Fraction(0) / Fraction(1, 4))
```

Why does $0 \div \frac{1}{4}$ have an answer, when $\frac{1}{4} \div 0$
does not?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way to see it</summary>

The cell prints 0. No quarter slices fit in no pizza, and 0 is a fine
count. For $\frac{1}{4} \div 0$, we ask how many slices of nothing fit
in a quarter. However many we take, they never fill it. So no number is
the answer. Here is one answer. Yours may be different and work too.

</details>

## 12. From earlier: a fraction of a fraction

From *Multiplying fractions: a fraction of a fraction*. What is
$\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{1}{2}$? Give its shortest name.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

$\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{10} = \frac{1}{5}$. Half of
two fifths is one fifth.

</details>
