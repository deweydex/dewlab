# Multiplying powers: joining two stacks — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Two stacks with the same base join into one
row, so we add the exponents. Try each problem before you open anything
under it. Choose numbers, shapes or letters in the box under the title.

Many worksheets write a dot for multiply: a² · a³ means a² × a³. This
page writes ×.

## 4. Two paths, one answer

Three stacks can join in two orders. Does the order change the answer?

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">

```question
id: two-paths-1--numbers
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path: 2² × 2⁴ is
{2⁶|2⁸|4⁶}.

Then 2⁶ × 2⁵ is
{2¹¹|2³⁰|4¹¹}.

Second path: 2⁴ × 2⁵ is
{2⁹|2²⁰|4⁹}.

Then 2² × 2⁹ is
{2¹¹|2¹⁸|4¹¹}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

```question
id: two-paths-1--squiggles
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path: △² × △⁴ is
{△⁶|△⁸|6△}.

Then △⁶ × △⁵ is
{△¹¹|△³⁰|11△}.

Second path: △⁴ × △⁵ is
{△⁹|△²⁰|9△}.

Then △² × △⁹ is
{△¹¹|△¹⁸|11△}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

```question
id: two-paths-1--letters
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path: g² × g⁴ is
{g⁶|g⁸|6g}.

Then g⁶ × g⁵ is
{g¹¹|g³⁰|11g}.

Second path: g⁴ × g⁵ is
{g⁹|g²⁰|9g}.

Then g² × g⁹ is
{g¹¹|g¹⁸|11g}.
```

</div>

Both paths end at the same power. Why do you think that is?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The long row has the same tiles, whichever two stacks join first:
2 + 4 + 5 = 11 tiles. So we can join three stacks at once, and add all
three exponents.

</details>

## 8. What went differently here?

Somebody found 2³ × 2⁴ and wrote 2¹². The cell below prints both.

```python exec
id: what-went-differently-1
print(2 ** 3 * 2 ** 4)
print(2 ** 12)
```

What did they do with the exponents? What would you say to them?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Write 2³ the long way. Then write 2⁴ the long way after it.
2. Count the twos in the row.
3. Where could 12 come from?

**Think about:** 3 × 4 = 12, and 3 + 4 = 7.

**Try this next:** somebody else wrote 4⁷. What did they do with the
bases?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

They multiplied the exponents: 3 × 4 = 12. The row has 3 twos and then
4 twos, which is 3 + 4 = 7 twos. So 2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷ = 128, and 2¹² is 4096.
Multiplying the exponents feels natural, because the question has a ×
in it. Written the long way, the row has 7 twos, not 12.

</details>

## 10. How many ways to twenty?

How many multiplications of powers simplify to e²⁰? Start with two
stacks, like e⁵ × e¹⁵. This cell lists every pair of exponents that
adds to 20. It lists each pair once, with
the smaller exponent first.

```python exec
id: how-many-ways-1
target = 20
for first in range(1, target):
    second = target - first
    if first <= second:
        print(first, "+", second)
```

How many pairs are there? What happens with three stacks, or four?
Change `target` to find the pairs for another exponent.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

There are 10 pairs, from 1 + 19 to 10 + 10. With three stacks there
are 33 ways, if the order does not matter. Now use two or more
stacks, as many as you like, and let the order matter. Then there are
524,287 ways.

</details>

## 11. A fraction or a negative number as the base

This one goes a step past the page. Does the rule still work when the
base is a fraction, like ½? Or a negative number, like −2?

The cell checks both. Its first line gets Python's fraction tool ready.

```python exec
id: a-fraction-or-a-negative-1
from fractions import Fraction

half = Fraction(1, 2)
print(half ** 2 * half ** 3, half ** 5)
print((-2) ** 2 * (-2) ** 3, (-2) ** 5)
```

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Yes. The first line prints 1/32 twice, and the second prints −32
twice. The long way still works: (−2)² is 4 and (−2)³ is −8, and
4 × −8 is −32. The rule only counts tiles. It does not care what number
is on each tile.

</details>

## 12. From earlier: two ways round

From *Powers: the long way and the short way*. Which is bigger, 2⁵ or
5²?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32, and 5² = 5 × 5 = 25. So 2⁵ is bigger.

</details>
