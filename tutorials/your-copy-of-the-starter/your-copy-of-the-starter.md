# Making your own copy of the starter

The website track begins with
[`portfolio_wad`](https://github.com/deweydex/portfolio_wad). It is a
small site of three pages. So we start with a site that already works,
and we change it and add to it. Before any exercise, you need your own copy of it. On
this page we:

- choose between two ways to get a copy
- choose between three ways to open that copy and edit it

## Two ways to get a copy

Open the `portfolio_wad` repository on GitHub. Near the top there are
two buttons that make a copy.

**Use this template** is GitHub's green button. It gives you a new
repository with a name you choose. The new repository starts its own
history with its first commit. It carries none of the history of
`portfolio_wad`, and GitHub shows only a small "generated from" note
under its name. This way suits a site you may want to keep, and show
people, after the course.

**Fork** is the other button, near the top of the same page. A *fork*
is your own copy of another person's repository. It also copies every
file into your own account. A fork keeps the history of the original,
and it stays connected to it, with a "forked from" note under its name.
Choose this way if you might want to bring in a later fix from
`portfolio_wad` itself.

Either way, you get your own repository. You can rename it now or
later. What name suits it best? A name like `portfolio_wad` says which
class the site is for. A plainer name suits a site you plan to keep
after the course ends.

Later pages call your copy "your fork", whichever button you pressed.

From now on, your copy behaves like any other repository:

- Changes you make in your copy never affect `portfolio_wad`.
- Later changes to `portfolio_wad` never appear in your copy, unless
  you ask for them.

This suits this course. We want a site that is entirely yours to change.

## Three ways to open it

Once you have your copy, there are three ways to start editing it. No
way is more correct than the others.

**Clone it.** Choose this if you have Git installed and you already
know how to use a terminal. A terminal is a window where you type
commands for the computer. To *clone* a repository is to download a
copy of it, with its history, that Git keeps connected to GitHub. You
edit the files on your computer. When you are ready, you push your
commits back up to GitHub.

**Download it.** Choose this if you want the files without Git. Press
GitHub's green **Code** button, then **Download ZIP**. You get a folder
of plain files to open in your editor. This folder has no connection to
GitHub. To put a changed file back into your repository, you upload it
on GitHub's website.

**Edit it in the browser.** This uses GitHub's own editor, from [Choosing an editor](tutorial:an-editor). You download nothing. Everything happens on
GitHub's website.

Not sure which to pick? The browser editor is the easiest place to
start. You can always clone or download the same repository later.

## What we have now

You now have your own copy of the starter, ready to open.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *fork* | Your own copy of another person's repository, which keeps its history and stays connected to it | your copy of `portfolio_wad` |
| *clone* | To download a copy of a repository, with its history, that Git keeps connected to GitHub | a `portfolio_wad` folder on your computer |

Next, [Publishing your site with GitHub Pages](tutorial:publish-it)
shows how to make your copy visible as a website. The starter's own
first exercise asks you to do this before you change anything else.
