# Scientific notation: powers of ten, from a virus to Voyager — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Scientific notation writes a number as one
digit, a point and more digits, times a power of ten. To multiply, we
multiply the front numbers and add the powers. Try each problem before
you open anything under it. Choose numbers, shapes or letters in the
box under the title.

## 1. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 3

Here are three numbers: 45 × 10⁵, 0.45 × 10⁷ and 4.5 × 10⁶. Which of
them are the same number?

- Only 45 × 10⁵ and 4.5 × 10⁶
  - Try 0.45 × 10⁷ the long way too: move the point seven places.
- None of them
  - The powers are different. But the front numbers are different too.
- All three
  - Each one is 4,500,000. Only 4.5 × 10⁶ is in scientific notation.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Write each one the long way. Start at the front number.
2. Move the point one place to the right for each step of the power.
3. Compare the three long numbers.

**Think about:** a bigger front number with a smaller power can make
the same number.

**Try this next:** is 450 × 10⁴ the same number too?

</details>

## 3. Fill the gap

This is the rule from *Multiplying powers: joining two stacks*.

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">

```question

(2 × 10⁴) × (4 × 10³) = 8 ×
{10⁷|10¹²|10¹}.

(2 × 10⁴) × (4 × 10^?) = 8 × 10⁹. The ? is
{5|13|36}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

```question
id: fill-the-gap-1--squiggles

$10^{\heartsuit} \times 10^{3} = 10^{?}$. The ? is
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

```question
id: fill-the-gap-1--letters

$10^{m} \times 10^{3} = 10^{?}$. The ? is
{m + 3|3m|3}.

{n|m|m + n}.
```

</div>

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The powers add when we multiply.
2. For a gap, ask: what do I add to the first power to get the answer?
3. Check by adding the two powers again.

**Think about:** the power counts the tens. Joining two stacks of tens
adds the counts.

**Try this next:** (5 × 10⁶) × (1 × 10^?) = 5 × 10¹⁰.

## 4. Match three ways

```question
id: match-three-ways-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Which of these is the same as "three ten-thousandths"?

- 0.0003 and 3 × 10⁻⁴
  - A ten-thousandth is 1/10,000, which is 10⁻⁴. Three of them is
    0.0003.
- 0.003 and 3 × 10⁻³
  - 0.003 is three thousandths. A ten-thousandth is ten times smaller.
- 30,000 and 3 × 10⁴
  - This is thirty thousand. A ten-thousandth is a tiny part of 1.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. A thousandth is 1/1000, which is 0.001.
2. A ten-thousandth is one step smaller: 0.0001.
3. Three of them is three times that.

**Think about:** 10,000 is 10⁴. So one ten-thousandth is 10⁻⁴.

**Try this next:** write "five millionths" in two ways.

</details>

## 7. What went differently here?

Here is a worked problem, with one step that went differently.

> (4 × 10⁵) × (3 × 10⁶) = 12 × 10³⁰ = 1.2 × 10³¹

What went differently? Check it with Python: `print(4e5 * 3e6)`.

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Look at the powers: 5 and 6. What happened to them?
2. Multiplying two powers of ten joins two stacks of tens.
3. How many tens are in the joined stack?

**Think about:** $10^5 \times 10^6$ is five tens, then six more tens.

**Try this next:** (2 × 10³) × (5 × 10⁴).

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The worked problem multiplied the powers: 5 × 6 = 30. Joining two
stacks adds them: 5 + 6 = 11. So it is 12 × 10¹¹, which is
1.2 × 10¹². Python prints 1200000000000.0, which is 1.2 × 10¹².

</details>

## 8. Two paths, one answer

How far does light travel in one minute? Here are two paths.

- **Metres first.** Light travels 299,792,458 m in a second. In 60
  seconds, it is 17,987,547,480 m. Then change to km.
- **Kilometres first.** Light travels about 3.00 × 10⁵ km in a second.
  Multiply by 60.

```question
id: two-paths-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

17,987,547,480 m is about 1.80 × 10¹⁰ m. A km is 10³ m, so that is
1.80 ×
{10⁷|10¹³|10³⁰}
km.

3.00 × 10⁵ × 60 is 1.8 ×
{10⁷|10⁶|10⁵}
km.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. To change metres to km, divide by 1000, which is 10³.
2. Dividing by 10³ takes 3 from the power: 10 − 3.
3. For the second path, 3.00 × 60 is 180, which is 1.8 × 10².

**Think about:** both paths measure the same light, for the same
minute.

**Try this next:** how far does light travel in one hour?

</details>

## 10. A container ship

Wikipedia says a 20-foot shipping container has about 33.1 cubic metres
of space inside. The largest container ships carry over 24,000
containers of this size.

```python exec
id: a-container-ship-1
container = 33.1            # cubic metres inside one container
containers_on_ship = 24000
ship = container * containers_on_ship
print(f"{ship:.2e}")
```

```predict
type: choice

Before you run it: about how many cubic metres can the ship carry?

- About 8 × 10⁵
- About 8 × 10⁴
  - 33.1 × 24,000 has 3.31 × 10¹ and 2.4 × 10⁴ in it. Add the powers.
- About 8 × 10⁴⁰
  - 10¹ × 10⁴ adds the powers. It does not multiply them.
```

It prints 7.94e+05: $7.94 \times 10^5$ cubic metres. Now guess the size
of a phone in its box, in metres. Say 0.20 × 0.10 × 0.05 m. (This is a
guess. Measure one if you can.) How many boxes fit in the ship? Change
the cell to find it.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The guessed box is 0.20 × 0.10 × 0.05 = 0.001 cubic metres. That is
10⁻³. Dividing by 10⁻³ is multiplying by 10³, so 7.94 × 10⁵ × 10³ =
7.94 × 10⁸ boxes. That is almost 800 million phones, if they fit with
no gaps.

</details>

## 11. A stretch: a tennis ball of atoms

Wikipedia says a caesium atom is about 260 picometres from its middle
to its edge. A *picometre* is 10⁻¹² m. So the atom is about
520 × 10⁻¹² m = 5.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m across. A tennis ball is about 6.7 cm,
or 6.7 × 10⁻² m, across.

How many caesium atoms fit in a row across a tennis ball?

```python exec
id: a-stretch-a-tennis-ball-1
ball = 6.7e-2       # metres across a tennis ball
atom = 5.2e-10      # metres across a caesium atom
across = ball / atom
print(f"{across:.2e}")
```

It prints 1.29e+08, about 10⁸ atoms in a row. Now think of a cube,
10⁸ atoms on each edge. How many atoms are in the cube?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. A cube 10 beads on each edge has 10 × 10 × 10 = 10³ beads.
2. A cube 10⁸ atoms on each edge has 10⁸ × 10⁸ × 10⁸ atoms.
3. Join the three stacks of tens.

**Think about:** this is *A power of a power: stacks of stacks*:
(10⁸)³.

**Try this next:** a cube 10⁴ on each edge.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

10⁸ × 10⁸ × 10⁸ = 10²⁴ atoms in the cube. The ball is round, so it
fills only about half of the cube around it. So 10²⁴ is a rough count,
not an exact one. It says how big the number is: about a million
million million million atoms.

</details>

## 13. From earlier: a cubic kilometre

From *A power of a power: stacks of stacks*. A kilometre is 10³
metres. A cube 1 km on each edge is 10³ m by 10³ m by 10³ m. How many
cubic metres is that?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

$(10^3)^3 = 10^3 \times 10^3 \times 10^3 = 10^9$ cubic metres. NASA
gives the Earth's volume in cubic kilometres, as
$1.08321 \times 10^{12}$. In cubic metres it is
$1.08321 \times 10^{12} \times 10^9 = 1.08321 \times 10^{21}$.

</details>
