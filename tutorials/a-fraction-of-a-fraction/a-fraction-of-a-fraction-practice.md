# Multiplying fractions: a fraction of a fraction — Practice

Small problems on one idea: a fraction of a fraction is the two
fractions multiplied. Try each problem before you open anything under

## 6. Close to 0, a half, or 1?

No working needed here. Picture the green block, and guess which of the
three amounts each one is closest to.

```question
id: close-to-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

9/10 × 9/10 is closest to {1|1/2|0}.

1/10 × 9/10 is closest to {0|1/2|1}.

1/2 × 99/100 is closest to {1/2|0|1}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. 9/10 is almost a whole. Most of the square is yellow.
2. Almost all of that is blue too. So is most of the square green?
3. For 1/2 × 99/100: take half, then take almost all of it.

**Think about:** taking almost all of something leaves almost the same
amount.

**Try this next:** is 1/10 × 1/10 closest to 0, a half or 1?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$\frac{9}{10} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{81}{100}$, which is closer to
1 than to a half. $\frac{1}{10} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{9}{100}$,
which is close to 0. $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{99}{100} =
\frac{99}{200}$, which is very close to a half.

</details>

## 7. Where does it go on the line?

Picture a number line from 0 to 2. Where does each answer go?

```question
id: on-the-line-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

1/3 × 1/3 is between {0 and 1/2|1/2 and 1|1 and 2}.

4 × 1/3 is between {1 and 2|0 and 1/2|1/2 and 1}.

1/2 × 3/2 is between {1/2 and 1|0 and 1/2|1 and 2}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Find each answer as a fraction first. 4 is the same as 4/1.
2. For 4/3: 3 thirds make 1. How many thirds are left over?
3. For 3/4: is it more than a half?

**Think about:** multiplying by a number bigger than 1, like 4 or 3/2,
does not make the answer smaller.

**Try this next:** where does 2 × 3/4 go?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{9}$, a little more than 0.
$4 \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{3}$, which is 1 and one more third.
$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{4}$, between a half and 1.

</details>

## 8. What went differently here?

Somebody found $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{5}$ like this:

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2 + 3}{3 + 5} = \frac{5}{8}$$

Is $\frac{5}{8}$ two thirds of $\frac{3}{5}$?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Two thirds of something is less than that something. So the answer
   must be less than 3/5.
2. Which is bigger, 5/8 or 3/5? A calculator, or `Fraction`, can tell
   you.
3. Now multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms.

**Think about:** which sign belongs in a fraction of a fraction, + or
×.

**Try this next:** find 3/4 × 2/5 by multiplying.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

No. The tops and the bottoms were added. A fraction of a fraction
multiplies them:

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2 \times 3}{3 \times 5} = \frac{6}{15} = \frac{2}{5}$$

There is a quick check, too. $\frac{5}{8}$ is 0.625 and $\frac{3}{5}$
is 0.6. So $\frac{5}{8}$ is more than $\frac{3}{5}$. Two thirds of
something cannot be more than that something.

</details>

## 9. Two paths, one answer

Here is $\frac{4}{6} \times \frac{3}{8}$, two ways.

- Path one: multiply first, then find the shortest name.
- Path two: give $\frac{4}{6}$ its shortest name first, then multiply.

Do the two paths meet at the same answer?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Path one: the tops make 4 × 3. The bottoms make 6 × 8.
2. Divide the top and the bottom of that answer by the same number,
   until you cannot divide any more.
3. Path two: 4/6 has a shorter name. Multiply that by 3/8.

**Think about:** which path has smaller numbers to handle.

**Try this next:** 2/4 × 6/9, both ways.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Path one: $\frac{4}{6} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{12}{48} =
\frac{1}{4}$.

Path two: $\frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$, and $\frac{2}{3} \times
\frac{3}{8} = \frac{6}{24} = \frac{1}{4}$.

Both paths give $\frac{1}{4}$. A shorter name is the same amount, so
the answer does not change.

</details>

## 11. Halves of halves

Take half of a whole. Then take half of that, and half of that, again
and again. The program below does it 10 times. Before you run it, can
you say what the third line will print?

```python exec
id: halves-of-halves-1
from fractions import Fraction

piece = Fraction(1)
for times in range(1, 11):
    piece = piece * Fraction(1, 2)
    print(times, piece)
```

This one goes past the page. After how many halves is the piece smaller
than $\frac{1}{1000}$?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Each line halves the piece, so each bottom number doubles.
2. Look down the bottom numbers. Which is the first one bigger than
   1000?
3. A bigger bottom number means a smaller piece.

**Think about:** where you have seen 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 before.

**Try this next:** change the program to take thirds instead. After how
many thirds is the piece smaller than $\frac{1}{1000}$?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

After 10 halves the piece is $\frac{1}{1024}$, which is smaller than
$\frac{1}{1000}$. After 9 halves it is $\frac{1}{512}$, which is still
bigger. This is the folded paper from *Powers: the long way and the
short way*: $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10} = \frac{1}{1024}$.

</details>

## 13. From earlier: every slice

From *Fractions: one whole pizza, many slices*. A pizza is cut into 6
slices. Each slice is $\frac{1}{6}$. What is $6 \times \frac{1}{6}$?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>
