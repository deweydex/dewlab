# Multiplying fractions: a fraction of a fraction

Here are two squares of the same size. The first square is cut into 2
*columns*. A column is a tall strip, from the top to the bottom. The
left column is yellow.

The second square is the same square, cut again. This time it is also
cut into 2 *rows*. A row is a wide strip, from one side to the other.
The top row is blue. Where the yellow and the blue cross, the piece is
green.

Are the colours hard to tell apart? Open **Settings**, then
**Appearance**, and turn on **Patterns in pictures**. Then the yellow
columns have stripes one way, the blue rows have stripes the other way,
and the green pieces, where they cross, have both.

<img src="half-of-a-half.svg" alt="Two squares of the same size. The first is cut into 2 columns, and the left column is yellow. It is labelled 'a half'. The second square is cut into 2 columns and 2 rows, which make 4 equal pieces. The left column is yellow and the top row is blue. The one piece where they cross, at the top left, is green. It is labelled 'half of that half'.">

```question
id: two-squares-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

The second square is cut into {4|2|3} equal pieces.

The number of green pieces is {1|2|3}.
```

## Half of a half

The yellow part is half of the square. The green part is half of the
yellow part. So the green part is half of a half.

```question
id: half-of-a-half-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 2

How much of the whole square is green?

- 1/2
  - The yellow part is one half. The green part is only half of that.
- 1/4
  - One green piece, out of 4 equal pieces in the whole square.
- 1/3
  - There is 1 green piece and there are 3 other pieces. The bottom
    number counts every piece, the green one too.
```

Half of a half is a quarter. You may have seen this before, with paper.
Fold a sheet in half, then in half again. Open it, and the folds cut it
into 4 equal pieces.

## "Of" means times

Half of 10 is 5. We can write this with a times sign:

$$\frac{1}{2} \times 10 = 5$$

The word *of*, between two numbers, means times. You already know this
from whole numbers: 3 bags of 4 apples is $3 \times 4 = 12$ apples.

So half of a half is written like this:

$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$$

```question
id: of-means-times-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

A third of 12 is {4|36|9}.

We write it 1/3 × 12 = {4|36|1/36}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. A third of something is one of 3 equal parts.
2. Put 12 things into 3 equal groups.
3. Count the things in one group.

**Think about:** "a third of 12" and "1/3 × 12" ask the same question.

**Try this next:** a quarter of 20. How would you write it with a times
sign?

</details>

## Half of a third

Here is a new pair of squares. The first square is cut into 3 columns.
The yellow part is now a third of the square. The second square is also
cut into 2 rows, and the top row is blue.

<img src="half-of-a-third.svg" alt="Two squares of the same size. The first is cut into 3 columns, and the left column is yellow. It is labelled 'a third'. The second square is cut into 3 columns and 2 rows, which make 6 equal pieces. The left column is yellow and the top row is blue. The one piece where they cross, at the top left, is green. It is labelled 'half of that third'.">

```question
id: half-of-a-third-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

The green part is half of the yellow part. How much of the whole square
is green?

- 1/6
  - One green piece, out of 6 equal pieces in the whole square.
- 1/5
  - There are 5 pieces that are not green. The bottom number counts
    every piece, the green one too.
- 1/3
  - One third is the yellow part. The green part is half of it.
- 1/2
  - The green part is half of the yellow part. The question asks about
    the whole square.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Look only at the second square.
2. Count all its pieces, row by row. Are they all the same size?
3. Count the green pieces.

**Think about:** the bottom number of a fraction counts all the equal
pieces in the whole.

**Try this next:** picture a square cut into 4 columns, with one column
yellow. Cut it across the middle. What is half of that quarter?

</details>

So half of a third is one sixth:

$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$$

## Where the 6 comes from

The second square has 3 columns, because of the third. It has 2 rows,
because of the half.

```question
id: where-the-6-comes-from-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

3 columns and 2 rows make {6|5|9} pieces in total.
```

The pieces in the whole square are $2 \times 3 = 6$. The bottom numbers
of the two fractions multiply together.

## Two thirds of three quarters

Now the first square is cut into 4 columns, and 3 of them are yellow.
The yellow part is three quarters. The second square is also cut into 3
rows, and 2 of them are blue. So the blue rows take two thirds of the
yellow part.

<img src="two-thirds-of-three-quarters.svg" alt="Two squares of the same size. The first is cut into 4 columns, and the 3 columns on the left are yellow. It is labelled 'three quarters'. The second square is cut into 4 columns and 3 rows, which make 12 equal pieces. The 3 left columns are yellow and the top 2 rows are blue. Where they cross, 6 pieces are green, in a block 3 pieces wide and 2 pieces high. It is labelled 'two thirds of that'.">

```question
id: two-thirds-of-three-quarters-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

The number of green pieces is {6|5|3}.

The number of pieces in the whole square is {12|7|4}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The green pieces make a block. Count the green pieces in its top
   row.
2. Count how many rows of green pieces there are.
3. For all the pieces, count the columns of the whole square, and then
   its rows.

**Think about:** you can count a block by its rows. You do not need to
count every piece one by one.

**Try this next:** how many pieces does a square have, if it is cut
into 5 columns and 4 rows?

</details>

So two thirds of three quarters is $\frac{6}{12}$. On *Equivalent
fractions: the same amount, different names*, we gave fractions shorter
names.

```question
id: two-thirds-of-three-quarters-2
type: fill-in-the-blank

6/12 is the same amount as {1/2|1/6|2/1}.
```

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$$

## The tops and the bottoms

Here is everything we counted on this page.

| A fraction of a fraction | Green pieces | All the pieces | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2}$ | $1 \times 1 = 1$ | $2 \times 2 = 4$ | $\frac{1}{4}$ |
| $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}$ | $1 \times 1 = 1$ | $2 \times 3 = 6$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ |
| $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4}$ | $2 \times 3 = 6$ | $3 \times 4 = 12$ | $\frac{6}{12}$ |

The green pieces always make a block. The two top numbers say how many
columns and how many rows the block has. So the tops multiply together.

The whole square is cut into columns and rows too. The two bottom
numbers say how many columns and how many rows. So the bottoms multiply
together.

```question
id: the-tops-and-the-bottoms-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Here is one without a picture. What is 3/5 × 1/2?

- 3/10
  - The tops make 3 × 1 = 3. The bottoms make 5 × 2 = 10.
- 4/7
  - This adds the tops and adds the bottoms. The pieces in a block
    multiply.
- 3/5
  - This is the first fraction. Taking half of it makes it smaller.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Picture a square cut into 5 columns, with 3 of them yellow.
2. Cut it again into 2 rows, with the top row blue.
3. Count the green block. Then count all the pieces.

**Think about:** which numbers in the question make the green block,
and which make the whole square.

**Try this next:** 2/5 × 1/3.

</details>

This is the rule for multiplying two fractions. Multiply the tops
together, and multiply the bottoms together. We did not start with the
rule. It came from counting pieces. If you forget it, draw the square
and count again.

## A machine that checks

Python has a tool for fractions, called `Fraction`. The first line below
gets it ready. In Python, the times sign is a star, `*`.

```python exec
id: a-machine-that-checks-1
from fractions import Fraction

print(Fraction(2, 3) * Fraction(3, 4))
```

```predict
type: choice

Before you run it: what will Python print for two thirds of three
quarters?

- 6/12
  - We counted 6 green pieces out of 12, so Python could print that.
- 1/2
  - `Fraction` always prints the shortest name for an amount.
- 5/7
  - That adds the tops and adds the bottoms.
```

Python prints 1/2. It found the same amount as our counting, and gave
it its shortest name. Now change the numbers and run it again. Can you
check half of a half, and half of a third?

## Smaller than both

Look at the answers again. Half of a half is a quarter, and a quarter
is smaller than a half.

```question
id: smaller-than-both-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2. How does 1/2 compare with 2/3 and with 3/4?

- It is smaller than both of them
  - 1/2 is less than 2/3, and less than 3/4.
- It is bigger than both of them
  - Multiplying often makes numbers bigger. Here we took a part of a
    part.
- It is between them
  - 1/2 is less than 2/3. Compare it with 3/4 too.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Picture a pizza. Is 2/3 of it more than half of it, or less?
2. Is 3/4 of it more than half, or less?
3. So where is 1/2, compared with each of them?

**Think about:** where the green part sits in the square.

**Try this next:** is 1/2 × 1/3 smaller than both 1/2 and 1/3?

</details>

The green part is inside the yellow part. It is inside the blue part
too. So when both fractions are smaller than 1, the answer is smaller
than both of them. Multiplying made the number smaller. Many people
find this strange, because multiplying so often makes a number bigger.
[Multiplying: a closer look at bigger and smaller](tutorial:does-multiplying-make-it-bigger)
has an experiment about it.

## Your rule, in your words

Before you read our version, say how to multiply two fractions in your
own words. Say where the rule comes from, too. Write it in the Notes
panel or on paper, or say it aloud.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way to say it</summary>

"Of" means times. To find a fraction of a fraction, cut a square one
way for the first fraction, and the other way for the second. The
green block is the answer. Multiply the tops to count the green pieces.
Multiply the bottoms to count all the pieces. Your way of saying it may
be clearer than ours.

</details>
