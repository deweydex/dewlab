# Solving equations: keeping it level — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Whatever we do to one side of an equation,
we do to the other side, and it stays level. We undo the steps that
built the box, one at a time, in the opposite order. Try each problem
before you open anything under it. Choose numbers, shapes or letters
in the list under the title. With numbers, the box is a ?.

<img src="two-boxes.svg" alt="A balance with its bar level. The left pan holds two closed boxes, each marked with a question mark, and 1 bead. The right pan holds 9 beads.">

## 1. Level or not?

Start each time from a level balance: □ + 6 = 10.

```question
id: level-or-not-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

We take 6 beads from both pans. The bar is
{level|tipped}.

We take 6 beads from the left pan only. The bar is
{tipped|level}.

We add 2 beads to both pans. The bar is
{level|tipped}.

We double what is on both pans. The bar is
{level|tipped}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The bar is level, so both pans weigh the same: 10 beads each.
2. After each move, find what each pan weighs now.
3. Are the two weights the same?

**Think about:** a move keeps the bar level when both pans change in
the same way.

**Try this next:** we add 2 beads to the left pan, and 3 to the right.
Level or tipped?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Both pans start at 10 beads. Taking 6 from both leaves 4 and 4: level.
Taking 6 from the left only leaves 4 against 10: tipped. Adding 2 to
both makes 12 and 12: level. Doubling both makes 20 and 20: level.

</details>

## 4. Undo a sharing

The beads in a box are shared into 2 equal groups. One of those groups
balances 6 beads. We write it like this:

$$\square \div 2 = 6$$

```question
id: undo-a-sharing-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

To undo dividing by 2, we
{multiply both sides by 2|divide both sides by 2|take 2 from both sides}.

The box holds
{12|3|4}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. One group balances 6 beads.
2. The box holds 2 groups of the same size.
3. Join the 2 groups again. How many beads is that?

**Think about:** which move joins equal groups again.

**Try this next:** □ ÷ 3 = 5.

</details>

## 5. Which step first?

$$3 \times \square + 4 = 19$$

```question
id: which-step-first-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Which move helps first?

- Take 4 from both sides
  - Adding 4 was the last building step, so it is the first to undo.
- Divide both sides by 3
  - The 4 loose beads would need sharing too. 19 beads do not share into 3
    equal groups of whole beads.
- Take 3 from both sides
  - The 3 counts the boxes. Nobody added 3 loose beads.
```

What does the box hold?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Take 4 from both sides: 3 × □ = 15. Share both sides into 3 equal
groups: □ = 5. Check it: 3 × 5 + 4 = 19.

</details>

## 7. What went differently here?

Here is a worked problem, with one step that went differently.

> 2 × □ + 6 = 20. Share both sides into 2 equal groups: □ + 6 = 10.
> Take 6 from both sides: □ = 4.

Check it by putting 4 back in. Which step went differently?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Put 4 in the box of 2 × □ + 6 = 20. What is the left side?
2. Now share the left pan into 2 equal groups. The 2 boxes go one to
   each group. Where do the 6 loose beads go?
3. What is in one group now?

**Think about:** sharing a pan shares everything on it, loose beads
too.

**Try this next:** solve 2 × □ + 6 = 20 by taking the 6 away first.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

With 4 in the box, 2 × 4 + 6 is 14, not 20. The sharing step went
differently. Sharing the left pan into 2 groups shares the 6 loose
beads too. Each group gets one box and 3 beads. So the line after
sharing is □ + 3 = 10, and the box holds 7. Check it: 2 × 7 + 6 = 20.

</details>

## 8. Two paths, one answer

Here is 2 × □ + 6 = 20 again, solved in two ways.

```question
id: two-paths-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path: take 6 from both sides. Then 2 × □ =
{14|26|10}.

Share both sides into 2 groups. The box holds
{7|13|5}.

Second path: share both sides into 2 groups first. Then □ +
{3|6|12}
= 10.

Take the loose beads from both sides. The box holds
{7|4|13}.
```

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Both paths put 7 in the box. The first path undoes the steps in the
opposite order, and needs no halving of loose beads. The second path
shares first, so it must share the loose beads too. Here, 6 beads share
into 2 groups of 3. If there were 5 loose beads, the second path would
give half beads.

</details>

## 9. Looks scary, is simple

```question
id: looks-scary-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

In □ + 3 − 3 = 9, the box holds
{9|3|15}.

In 7 × □ ÷ 7 = 6, the box holds
{6|42|1}.

In 1000 × □ + 1 = 5001, the box holds
{5|5000|5001000}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. In □ + 3 − 3, we add 3, and then take 3 away. What changed?
2. In 7 × □ ÷ 7, we multiply by 7, and then divide by 7. What changed?
3. For the last one, undo "add 1" first. Then undo "times 1000".

**Think about:** a step and its undoing, side by side, do nothing at
all.

**Try this next:** □ × 100 ÷ 100 + 5 − 5 = 8.

</details>

## 10. A checking machine

Here is the checking machine from the page. It puts a number in the
box and builds the left side. In Python, `*` means times, and `==`
asks "are these the same?"

```python exec
id: a-checking-machine-1
box = 7
left_side = 2 * box + 6
right_side = 20

print(left_side, right_side)
print(left_side == right_side)
```

It prints `20 20`, and then `True`. Change the box, and the two sides,
to check your answers to other problems on this page.

## 11. A stretch: boxes on both pans

$$5 \times \square + 1 = 2 \times \square + 13$$

There are boxes on both pans. What does the box hold?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The right pan has 2 boxes. Take 2 boxes from both pans.
2. How many boxes are still on the left pan?
3. Now it is like problem 5. Undo one step at a time.

**Think about:** a box weighs the same on either pan, even if we do
not know what it holds.

**Try this next:** 4 × □ + 2 = □ + 11.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Take 2 boxes from both pans: 3 × □ + 1 = 13. Take 1 bead from both
sides: 3 × □ = 12. Share both sides into 3 groups: □ = 4. Check it:
5 × 4 + 1 is 21, and 2 × 4 + 13 is 21 too.

</details>
