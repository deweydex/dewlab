# The Drake equation: narrowing it down, one fraction at a time — Practice

Small problems on one idea. A chain starts with a count, and multiplies
it by one fraction after another. Each fraction keeps a part. Try each
problem before you open anything under it. Choose numbers, shapes or
letters in the box under the title.

<img src="a-town.svg" alt="Four bars, one under another, each shorter than the one above. The top bar is labelled start, and its count is 1000. The second bar is labelled like tea, times 1/2, and its count is 500. The third bar is labelled have a bike, times 1/5, and its count is 100. The last bar is labelled can juggle, times 1/10, and its count is 10.">

## 1. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 4

Three of these chains end at the same count. Which one is different?

- 1000 × 1/2 × 1/5
  - Half of 1000 is 500, and a fifth of 500 is 100.
- 1000 × 1/5 × 1/2
  - A fifth of 1000 is 200, and half of 200 is 100. The order changed,
    and the end did not.
- 1000 × 1/10
  - 1/2 × 1/5 is 1/10. So this is the same chain in one step: 100.
- 1000 × 1/7
  - 2 + 5 = 7. This adds the bottoms. A seventh of 1000 is about 143.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Find the end of the first chain, one step at a time.
2. Do the same for the second chain.
3. For the last two, find a tenth of 1000, and a seventh of 1000.

**Think about:** what 1/2 × 1/5 is, as one fraction.

**Try this next:** is 1000 × 1/4 × 1/25 the same as 1000 × 1/100?

</details>

## 4. Closer to 0, a half, or 1?

Each line multiplies fractions only. There is no start count. Guess
first, then check.

```question
id: closer-to-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

1/2 × 1/5 × 1/10 is
{closer to 0|closer to a half|closer to 1}.

3/4 × 2/3 is
{closer to a half|closer to 0|closer to 1}.

9/10 × 9/10 is
{closer to 1|closer to a half|closer to 0}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Multiply the tops, then the bottoms.
2. Compare the answer with 1/2. Is the top about half the bottom?
3. Is the top nearly as big as the bottom?

**Think about:** a fraction close to 1 keeps nearly everything. A tiny
fraction keeps nearly nothing.

**Try this next:** is 1/2 × 1/2 closer to 0, a half, or 1?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

- 1/2 × 1/5 × 1/10 = 1/100. That is very close to 0.
- 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12, which is exactly one half.
- 9/10 × 9/10 = 81/100. That is closer to 1.

</details>

## 5. Match three ways

```question

In symbols, the chain is
{600 × 1/3 × 1/2|600 × 1/5|600 × 3 × 2}.

At the end, the count is
{100|120|3600}
students.

As one fraction of the school, that is
{1/6|1/5|1/3}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. "A third of them" is × 1/3.
2. "Half of those" is × 1/2.
3. A third of 600 is 200. Half of 200 is what?

**Think about:** 1/3 × 1/2, as one fraction.

**Try this next:** a quarter of the walkers have a dog. How many is

## 6. Two paths, one answer

Here is the town chain again:

$$1000 \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{5} \times \frac{1}{10}$$

The first path goes one bar at a time: 500, then 100, then 10. Can you
find a second path? Multiply the three fractions first, and use the
start count last.

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Multiply the tops: 1 × 1 × 1.
2. Multiply the bottoms: 2 × 5 × 10.
3. Now take that fraction of 1000.

**Think about:** why the two paths must agree.

**Try this next:** do the same for 600 × 1/3 × 1/2.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The fractions together are 1/2 × 1/5 × 1/10 = 1/100. One hundredth of
1000 is 10. The first path gave 10 too. Both paths multiply the same
four numbers, only in a different order.

</details>

## 7. What went differently here?

Somebody found the count of the town that likes tea and has a bike
like this. "The fractions are 1/2 and 1/5. 2 + 5 = 7. So the count is
1000 × 1/7, which is about 143." The picture says 100. Which step
changed the amount?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Find half of 1000. Then find a fifth of that.
2. Now multiply 1/2 × 1/5, tops and bottoms.
3. Where did "2 + 5" come from?

**Think about:** each fraction cuts the bar above it again. Cutting
again multiplies the pieces.

**Try this next:** somebody wrote 1/2 × 1/5 = 2/7. What would you show
them?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The step "2 + 5 = 7" changed the amount. A fraction of a fraction
multiplies the bottoms: 1/2 × 1/5 = 1/10. A tenth of 1000 is 100, as the
picture shows. Adding the bottoms feels natural, because adding is the
first thing we learn to do with two numbers. Here is one answer. Yours
may be different and work too.

</details>

## 10. Your own Drake guesses

Here is Drake's chain with his smallest guesses, except the last one.
Can you find a number of years for `years_sending` that makes the
answer exactly 1: our world alone?

```python exec
id: your-own-drake-guesses-1
from fractions import Fraction

with_planets = Fraction(1, 5)
sends_signals = Fraction(1, 10)
years_sending = 1000
# The 1s are the steps Drake guessed as 1.
print(1 * with_planets * 1 * 1 * 1 * sends_signals * years_sending)
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Without the last step, the chain is 1/5 × 1/10.
2. Multiply those two fractions.
3. What number times that fraction makes 1?

**Think about:** the partner of a fraction, turned over.

**Try this next:** with `years_sending = 1000`, which value of
`sends_signals` makes the answer 1?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Without the last step, the chain is 1/5 × 1/10 = 1/50. Its partner is
50. With `years_sending = 50`, the cell prints 1. Here is one answer.
Yours may be different and work too.

</details>

## 11. The whole Universe

This one is a stretch. Frank and Sullivan also counted the whole
*observable Universe*: all the space we can see with telescopes. They
use $2 \times 10^{22}$ stars. For every five stars, there is about one
planet in a habitable zone. The cell finds that count, and its reciprocal.

```python exec
id: the-whole-universe-1
from fractions import Fraction

stars = 2 * 10 ** 22
planets = stars * Fraction(1, 5)
print(planets)
print(1 / planets)
print(float(1 / planets))
```

What value of $f_{bt}$ makes $A = 1$ for the whole observable Universe?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The first line printed is the count of planets, $N_{ast}$.
2. For $A = 1$, $f_{bt}$ is one over that count.
3. `float` writes the fraction as a decimal. `e-22` means times
   $10^{-22}$.

**Think about:** the Milky Way gave one in 60 billion. Is the Universe's
fraction bigger or smaller?

**Try this next:** how many zeros does the first line have?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The planets are 4,000,000,000,000,000,000,000, which is $4 \times
10^{21}$. For $A = 1$, $f_{bt}$ is one over that: $2.5 \times
10^{-22}$. This is the number in Frank and Sullivan's Table 1. It is
much smaller than one in 60 billion, because there are far more
planets.

</details>
