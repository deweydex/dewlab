# Coordinates: two lines at right angles — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Two numbers give a place on a page: steps
across first, then steps up. Try each problem before you open anything
under it. There is no hurry, and no score. Some problems come with
numbers, shapes or letters. Choose the way you like in the box under
the title.

## 1. Read the picture

```question
id: read-the-picture-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

A is at
{(2, 3)|(3, 2)|(2, −3)}.

B is at
{(−3, 2)|(2, −3)|(3, 2)}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Start at 0, in the middle of the grid.
2. Count the steps across to the dot. Steps to the left give a
   negative number.
3. Count the steps up to the dot. Steps down give a negative number.

**Think about:** the across number always comes first.

**Try this next:** where is C?

</details>

## 2. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 2

Here are three things: "3 steps across and 1 step up", (1, 3), and
(3, 1). Which two are the same place?

- "3 steps across and 1 step up", and (1, 3)
  - (1, 3) is 1 step across and 3 steps up.
- "3 steps across and 1 step up", and (3, 1)
  - Across comes first: 3. Up comes second: 1.
- (1, 3) and (3, 1)
  - They have the same two numbers, in a different order. The order
    tells the direction.
```

## 6. Continue the pattern

```question
id: continue-the-pattern-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

(1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 3). The next point is
{(4, 4)|(3, 4)|(4, 3)}.

(0, 5), (1, 4), (2, 3). The next point is
{(3, 2)|(3, 4)|(2, 2)}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Look at the first numbers only. What happens to them each time?
2. Now look at the second numbers only.
3. Continue each list one more step.

**Think about:** in the second pattern, one number goes up and the
other goes down.

**Try this next:** put the points of each pattern on a grid, on paper.
What shape do they make?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

In the first pattern, both numbers go up by 1 each time. So the next
point is (4, 4). In the second pattern, the first number goes up by 1,
and the second goes down by 1. So the next point is (3, 2). On a grid,
each pattern makes a straight row of dots. A later page looks at
patterns like these as pictures.

</details>

## 8. Match three ways

```question
id: match-three-ways-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Which point is "2 steps to the left, then 4 steps down"?

- (−2, −4)
  - Left gives a negative first number. Down gives a negative second
    number.
- (−4, −2)
  - This is 4 steps to the left and 2 steps down. The across number
    comes first.
- (2, −4)
  - This is 2 steps to the right, not to the left.
```

A third way to see it is on a grid: put your finger on 0, move 2
squares to the left, and then 4 squares down.

## 9. What went differently here?

Somebody was asked to put a dot at (1, 4). They put it 4 steps across
and 1 step up. What went differently? Where is their dot?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Which number in (1, 4) says the steps across?
2. How many steps across did they take?
3. Write their dot as two numbers in brackets.

**Think about:** the two numbers are the same, but the order is not.

**Try this next:** somebody put a dot for (−2, 3) at 3 steps to the
left and 2 steps up. Where did they put it?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

They used the numbers in the other order: up first, then across. Their
dot is at (4, 1). The dot for (1, 4) is 1 step across and 4 steps up.
It is high on the left, and their dot is low on the right.

</details>

## 10. Two paths, one answer

Start at 0. One path takes 3 steps to the right, and then 2 steps up.
Another path takes 2 steps up first, and then 3 steps to the right.

```question
id: two-paths-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

The first path ends at
{(3, 2)|(2, 3)|(5, 0)}.

The second path ends at
{(3, 2)|(2, 3)|(0, 5)}.
```

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Both paths end at (3, 2). Each path takes 3 steps across and 2 steps
up in total. The order of the steps does not change the place. The
order of the two *numbers* in the brackets does: across is always
written first.

</details>

## 11. A stretch: the last corner

A square has three corners at (1, 1), (4, 1) and (4, 4). Where is the
fourth corner? How long is each side of the square?

The program below joins the points in order. Change the last point,
and run it again. Can you add one more point, so that the square
closes?

```python exec
id: the-last-corner-1
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

across = [1, 4, 4, 0]
up = [1, 1, 4, 0]

plt.figure(figsize=(4, 4))
plt.plot(across, up, "o-")
plt.xticks(range(-1, 7))
plt.yticks(range(-1, 7))
plt.grid()
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. From (1, 1) to (4, 1), how many steps across is it?
2. From (4, 1) to (4, 4), how many steps up is it?
3. The fourth corner is above (1, 1), as far up as (4, 4).

**Think about:** all four sides of a square are the same length.

**Try this next:** a square has corners at (−2, −2), (1, −2) and
(1, 1). Where is the fourth?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The fourth corner is (1, 4). Each side is 3 steps long. From (1, 1) to
(4, 1) is 3 steps across, and from (4, 1) to (4, 4) is 3 steps up. In
the cell, the last point becomes `1` in `across` and `4` in `up`. Add
one more point at (1, 1) to draw the last side.

</details>
