# Dividing powers: crossing out pairs — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Each tile on the bottom crosses out one
tile on the top. So we take the bottom exponent away from the top one.
Try each problem before you open anything under it. Choose numbers,
shapes or letters in the box under the title.

In every problem here, the top has more tiles than the bottom. Problem
11 goes one step further.

## 4. Two on the bottom: two paths

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

are crossed out, and 4 are left.

## 8. What went differently here?

Somebody found 2⁶/2² and wrote 2³. The cell below prints both.

```python exec
id: what-went-differently-1
print(2 ** 6 / 2 ** 2)
print(2 ** 3)
```

What did they do with the exponents?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Write 2⁶ the long way on the top, and 2² under it.
2. Cross out the pairs. How many twos are left on the top?
3. Where could 3 come from?

**Think about:** 6 ÷ 2 = 3, and 6 − 2 = 4.

**Try this next:** somebody else wrote 1⁴. What did they do with the
bases?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

They divided the exponents: 6 ÷ 2 = 3. But the bottom crosses out only
2 of the 6 twos on the top, and 4 are left. So 2⁶/2² = 2⁴ = 16. The
cell prints 16.0 for the division, and 8 for 2³. Dividing the exponents
feels natural, because the question is a division.

</details>

## 10. What leaves three?

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">

What division of two powers of 2 leaves 2³? Can you find three

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

What division of two powers of f leaves f³? Can you find three
different ones? Can you find one where the top is two stacks?

</div>

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. After the crossing out, 3 tiles are left on the top.
2. So the top has 3 more tiles than the bottom.
3. Choose any bottom. How many tiles does the top need?

**Think about:** how many answers there could be.

**Try this next:** a division that leaves f³, where every exponent is
bigger than 100.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

f⁴/f¹, f⁵/f² and f¹⁰⁰/f⁹⁷ all leave f³. The top needs 3 more tiles than
the bottom, so there is no end to the list. With two stacks on the top:
(f⁴ × f⁵)/f⁶. The top joins into f⁹, and 9 − 6 = 3. Here is one answer.
Yours may be different and work too.

</details>

## 13. From earlier: crossing out in a fraction

From *Equivalent fractions: the same amount, different names*. What is
the simplest name for $\frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 2}$? Where have you
seen something like this on this page?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$\frac{3}{4}$. The 2 on the top and the 2 on the bottom make
$\frac{2}{2} = 1$, so they cross out as a pair. It is the same move as
crossing out a pair of hearts.
