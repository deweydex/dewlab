# Mixed problems: powers

These problems use all the pages on powers. They are mixed on purpose.
A problem does not say which page it comes from. It does not say which
rule it needs, either. Choosing the rule is part of the problem. Most
problems need one rule, and some need two.

When you are not sure, write the power the long way, and count. The
long way works for every problem here. Try each problem before you
open anything under it. There is no hurry, and no score. Under most
problems, "stuck? here are some steps" says which page the idea comes
from. Choose numbers, shapes or letters in the box under the title.

## 7. What went differently here?

Here is a worked line. One step used a different rule from the one it
needed.

$$\left(\heartsuit^2 \times \heartsuit^4\right)^3 = \left(\heartsuit^8\right)^3 = \heartsuit^{24}$$

Which step went differently? The cell puts 2 in place of the heart. It
finds the start of the line, then $2^{24}$, then $2^{18}$.

```python exec

```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

   and *A power of a power: stacks of stacks*.
2. Look inside the brackets first. ♡² × ♡⁴ is two stacks side by side.
   Write them the long way. How many hearts are in the row?
3. Then there are 3 boxes of that row.

**Try this next:** (♡³ × ♡)².

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one answer</summary>

exponents, and that step has boxes. From $\heartsuit^6$, it gives
$\left(\heartsuit^6\right)^3 = \heartsuit^{18}$.

The cell agrees. The first line and the last line both print 262144.

## 8. What went differently here?

Somebody found $5^0 \times 5^3$ like this:

> $5^0$ is 0. Zero times any number is 0. So the answer is 0.

Can you find $5^0 \times 5^3$ in two other ways? Do your two ways agree
with each other?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *The zero power: when everything cancels*
   and *Multiplying powers: joining two stacks*.
2. One way: join the two stacks. Add the exponents 0 and 3.
3. Another way: 5⁰ comes from a division like 5³/5³. What is that
   division? Multiply the answer by 5³.

**Think about:** a stack of 0 tiles adds no tiles to the row. Does it
make the row disappear?

**Try this next:** what is 5⁰ + 5⁰?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one answer</summary>

Joining the stacks gives $5^{0 + 3} = 5^3 = 125$. The zero power gives
$5^0 = 1$, and $1 \times 5^3 = 125$. The two ways agree. The step that
went differently is "$5^0$ is 0". Every tile in $5^3/5^3$ cancels, and
each pair is worth 1. So $5^0 = 1$. Here is one answer. Yours may be
different and work too.

</details>

## 10. Two paths, one answer

Here is a power of a fraction:

$$\left(\frac{\heartsuit^6}{\heartsuit^2}\right)^3$$

Find it in two ways.

- **Inside first.** Cross out pairs inside the brackets. Then use the
  3.
- **Outside first.** The 3 reaches the top and the bottom. Find
  $\left(\heartsuit^6\right)^3$ and $\left(\heartsuit^2\right)^3$. Then
  cross out pairs.

Do the two paths meet?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Dividing powers: crossing out pairs* and
   *A power of a power: stacks of stacks*. From *Powers: the long way
   and the short way*, a power of a fraction reaches the top and the
   bottom.
2. Inside first: ♡⁶/♡² leaves ♡⁴. Then there are 3 boxes of ♡⁴.
3. Outside first: 3 boxes of 6 on the top, and 3 boxes of 2 on the
   bottom. Then cross out.

**Think about:** why the two paths must meet.

**Try this next:** (♡⁸/♡⁶)⁵, in both ways.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Inside first: $\frac{\heartsuit^6}{\heartsuit^2} = \heartsuit^4$, and
$\left(\heartsuit^4\right)^3 = \heartsuit^{12}$.

Outside first: $\frac{\heartsuit^{18}}{\heartsuit^6} =
\heartsuit^{12}$.

Both paths give $\heartsuit^{12}$. With 2 for the heart, both are 4096.

</details>

## 11. Two paths: a negative power in a row

Here is $\heartsuit^5 \times \heartsuit^{-2}$. Find it in two ways.

- **Join.** Add the exponents, as for any two stacks side by side.
- **Cross out.** Write $\heartsuit^{-2}$ as one over a power first.
  Then cross out pairs.

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Multiplying powers: joining two stacks*,
   *Negative powers: more on the bottom* and *Dividing powers: crossing
   out pairs*.
2. For the first path, 5 + (−2): start at 5 on a number line, and take
   away 2, one step at a time.
3. For the second path, ♡⁻² is 1/♡². So ♡⁵ × ♡⁻² is ♡⁵ over ♡².

**Think about:** multiplying by one over a number is the same as
dividing by that number.

**Try this next:** 2⁵ × 2⁻², with numbers.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Join: $5 + (-2) = 3$, so the answer is $\heartsuit^3$.

Cross out: $\heartsuit^5 \times \frac{1}{\heartsuit^2} =
\frac{\heartsuit^5}{\heartsuit^2} = \heartsuit^3$.

The two paths meet. A negative power in a row takes tiles away.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

- The shelf: $10^2 \times 10^3 = 10^5$ beads.
- The bars: $10^5 / 10^1 = 10^4$ bars.
- The big cube: $10^3 \times 10^3 = 10^6$ beads. That is also
  $\left(10^3\right)^2$: 2 boxes of 3 tens. Two rules, one answer.
- The squares: $10^6 / 10^2 = 10^4$ squares.

</details>

## 16. A stretch: millimetres in a kilometre

A millimetre is $10^{-3}$ metres. A kilometre is $10^3$ metres. So the
number of millimetres in a kilometre is

$$\frac{10^3}{10^{-3}}$$

How many is that? Can you find it in two ways?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Negative powers: more on the bottom*,
   *Dividing fractions: how many fit?* and *Multiplying powers: joining
   two stacks*.
2. First way: 10⁻³ is 1/10³. Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by
   it turned over. What is 1/10³ turned over?
3. Second way: one metre holds 1000 millimetres. How many metres are in
   a kilometre?

**Think about:** the subtracting rule gives 3 − (−3). What must that
be, if both ways agree?

**Try this next:** a centimetre is 10⁻² metres. How many centimetres
are in a kilometre?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

First way: $\frac{10^3}{10^{-3}} = 10^3 \times 10^3 = 10^6$.

Second way: a kilometre is 1000 metres, and each metre is 1000
millimetres. That is $1000 \times 1000 = 1{,}000{,}000$ millimetres.

Both ways give a million, which is $10^6$. So $3 - (-3)$ must be 6.
Taking away a negative number adds.

</details>

## 17. A stretch: a negative power of a negative power

What is $\left(10^{-2}\right)^{-3}$? Guess first. Is it a very big
number, or a very small one? Then try two ways.

- **Boxes.** Multiply the two exponents: −2 × −3.
- **One over.** $10^{-2}$ is $\frac{1}{100}$. Then
  $\left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^{-3}$ is one over
  $\left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^3$.

The cell checks the second way. Its first line gets Python's fraction
tool ready.

```python exec
id: a-stretch-a-negative-power-1
from fractions import Fraction

hundredth = Fraction(1, 100)
print(hundredth ** -3)
print(Fraction(10) ** 6)
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *A power of a power: stacks of stacks* and
   *Negative powers: more on the bottom*.
2. (1/100)³ is 1/100 × 1/100 × 1/100. Multiply the tops and the
   bottoms.
3. One over a very small fraction asks how many of it fit in 1.

**Think about:** both ways must agree. So what must −2 × −3 be?

**Try this next:** what is (10⁻¹)⁻²?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Boxes: $-2 \times -3 = 6$, so it is $10^6$.

One over: $\left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{1000000}$. One over
that is 1,000,000, which is $10^6$. The cell prints 1000000 twice. It
is a very big number.

</details>
