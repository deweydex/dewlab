# The number line: whole numbers, fractions and negatives — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Every number has a place on one line.
Negative numbers are to the left of 0, and a number further to the
right is bigger. Try each problem before you open anything under it.
There is no hurry, and no score. Some problems come with numbers,
shapes or letters. Choose the way you like in the box under the title.

<img src="some-points.svg" alt="A number line from −5 to 5. Each whole number has a tick and a label, and a small tick sits halfway between each pair of whole numbers. Four dots sit on the line, labelled −3, −1/2, 2 and 7/2.">

## 1. Where on the line?

```question
id: where-on-the-line-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

5/2 goes between
{2 and 3|5 and 6|0 and 1}.

−2 goes
{2 steps to the left of 0|2 steps to the right of 0|on 0}.

−3/2 goes between
{−2 and −1|1 and 2|−1 and 0}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. 5/2 is 5 halves. 4 halves make 2 wholes. One half is left over.
2. −3/2 is the opposite of 3/2. First find 3/2 on the right of 0.
3. Its opposite is as far from 0, on the left.

**Think about:** 3/2 is one and a half.

**Try this next:** where does −7/2 go?

</details>

## 2. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Here are three things: 4 degrees below zero, the number −4, and 4
steps to the right of 0. Which two are the same number?

- 4 degrees below zero, and −4
  - Below zero is to the left of 0, 4 steps away.
- −4, and 4 steps to the right of 0
  - These are opposites. They are the same distance from 0, on
    different sides.
- 4 degrees below zero, and 4 steps to the right of 0
  - Both are 4 away from 0. One is below zero, and the other is above.
```

## 7. Match three ways

```question
id: match-three-ways-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

A lift is on floor 1. It goes 3 floors down, to floor −2. Which line
of maths says the same thing?

- 1 − 3 = −2
  - Start at 1, and take 3 steps to the left.
- 1 + 3 = 4
  - Adding moves to the right, and the lift went down.
- 3 − 1 = 2
  - This starts at 3. The lift started at floor 1.
```

A third way to see it is the number line: put your finger on 1, and
take 3 steps to the left.

## 8. What went differently here?

Somebody put four numbers in order, from the smallest to the biggest:

> −1, −3, −5, 2

The 2 is last. It is the only number on the right of 0. What went
differently with the other
three? Python can check. `sorted` puts a list of numbers in order, from
the smallest to the biggest.

```python exec
id: what-went-differently-1
print(sorted([-1, -3, -5, 2]))
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Find −1, −3 and −5 on the line.
2. Which of them is furthest to the left? That one is the smallest.
3. Which is closest to 0?

**Think about:** 5 is bigger than 1, but −5 is smaller than −1.

**Try this next:** put −2, 4, −7 and 0 in order.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The order is −5, −3, −1, 2. The cell prints `[-5, -3, -1, 2]`. The
worked line put the negative numbers in the order of their opposites: 1,
3, 5. On the left of 0, the order is the other way. −5 is furthest to
the left, so it is the smallest.

</details>

## 9. Two paths, one answer

How far is it from −3 to 4?

```question
id: two-paths-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path: count every step, from −3 to 4. That is
{7|1|4}
steps.

Second path: from −3 to 0 is
{3|−3|4}
steps.

From 0 to 4 is
{4|3|1}
steps.

In total, that is
{7|1|12}
steps.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. For the first path, say each number aloud: −2, −1, 0, 1, …
2. Count one step for each number you say, until you say 4.
3. For the second path, stop at 0 on the way, and count twice.

**Think about:** why both paths must give the same count.

**Try this next:** how far is it from −6 to 2?

</details>

## 11. A stretch: halfway between

Which number is halfway between −3 and 5?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Find the distance from −3 to 5. Count the steps.
2. Halve that distance.
3. Start at −3, and move that many steps to the right.

**Think about:** halfway is as far from −3 as it is from 5.

**Try this next:** which number is halfway between −6 and 2?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

From −3 to 5 is 8 steps: 3 steps to 0, then 5 more. Half of 8 is 4.
Start at −3 and move 4 steps to the right: −2, −1, 0, 1. So 1 is
halfway. Check: from 1 to 5 is 4 steps too.

</details>
