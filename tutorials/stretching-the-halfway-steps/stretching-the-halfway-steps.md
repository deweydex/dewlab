# Stretching the halfway steps: fractional exponents, the top and the bottom

Here is a line with the numbers 1, 2, 4 and 8 on it. Each curve is a
*hop*{.term}, and a hop multiplies. The big curve on top is one hop of ×8.
Under it, three small hops each multiply by 2. Look at the small hops
before you answer.

<img src="thirds-of-eight.svg" alt="A number line with stops at 1, 2, 4 and 8. A big arc labelled ×8 hops from 1 to 8. Under it, three small arcs labelled ×2 hop from 1 to 2, from 2 to 4 and from 4 to 8. Under the stops are the fractions 0, 1/3, 2/3 and 1, with the words: how far along, in whole hops.">

```question
id: three-small-hops-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Start at 1. Where do two small hops land?

- 4
  - 1 × 2 is 2, and 2 × 2 is 4.
- 2
  - 2 is where one small hop lands. The question asks about two.
- 5 and a third
  - Two thirds of 8 is 5 and a third. But hops multiply. Is there a
    stop at 5 and a third on the line?
- 6
  - 2 + 2 + 2 is 6. That adds three 2s. Hops multiply.
```

## How far along

Now look under the line. Under each stop, a small number says how far
along the big hop it is. 1 is at the start, so it is 0 of the way
along. 8 is at the end of the big hop, so it is 1 whole hop along.

4 has $\frac{2}{3}$ under it. Two small hops out of three take us two
thirds of the way along the big hop.

```question
id: how-far-along-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

2 is
{1/3|2/3|1/2}
of the way along the big hop.

8 is
{1|3|8}
whole hop along.
```

## The bottom of the fraction

In $\frac{2}{3}$, the bottom number is 3. It says the big hop is cut
into 3 equal small hops. It is like a pizza cut into 3 equal slices.

Three equal small hops make one hop of ×8. An earlier page had a name
for this small hop, edge(8). It is the edge of a cube of 8 beads. The cube is 2 beads
wide, 2 high and 2 deep, because 2 × 2 × 2 is 8.

```question
id: the-bottom-of-the-fraction-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

The bottom, 3, says one whole hop is cut into
{3|2|8}
small hops.

Each small hop is edge(8), which is
{2|3|8 ÷ 3}.
```

## The top of the fraction

In $\frac{2}{3}$, the top number is 2. It says how many small hops to
take. Two small hops of ×2 land on 4. So two thirds of the way along a
hop of ×8 is 4.

Now try a bigger hop. One hop of ×27 is cut into thirds. A cube of 27
beads is 3 beads on each edge, so edge(27) is 3.

```question
id: the-top-of-the-fraction-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Start at 1. Where is two thirds of the way along a hop of ×27?

- 9
  - Two small hops of ×3: 1 × 3 is 3, and 3 × 3 is 9.
- 18
  - 27 ÷ 3 × 2 is 18. That is two thirds of 27, as a distance. But
    each small hop multiplies.
- 3
  - 3 is where one small hop lands. The top of the fraction is 2.
- 6
  - 3 + 3 is 6. That adds two small hops. Hops multiply.
```

## Two paths to one answer

There is a second path to two thirds of the way along a hop of ×8.

- **Path 1.** Find the small hop first, and then take two of them.
  edge(8) is 2, and 2 × 2 is 4.
- **Path 2.** Take two whole hops first, and then find a third of
  them. Two whole hops of ×8 multiply by 8 × 8, which is 64. A third of
  that is edge(64).

4 × 4 × 4 is 64, so edge(64) is 4. The two paths land in the same
place. Why?

```question
id: two-paths-to-one-answer-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

Each whole hop of ×8 is 3 small hops of ×2. So two whole hops are
{6|2|16}
small hops.

A third of those is
{2|3|6}
small hops.
```

## Finding the small hop by trying

Python is a language for computers. It can find a small hop by trying
whole numbers: 1, then 2, then 3, and so on. In Python, `**` means a
power, so `2 ** 3` is $2^3$. This program tries each guess, done
`parts` times, until it reaches the big hop.

```python exec
id: finding-the-small-hop-by-trying-1
def small_hop(big_hop, parts):
    guess = 1
    while guess ** parts < big_hop:
        guess = guess + 1
    return guess

hop = small_hop(8, 3)
print(hop)
print(hop ** 2)
```

```predict
type: number

Before you run it: the first line prints the small hop, edge(8). The
last line takes two small hops. What will the last line print?
```

It prints 2, and then 4. Two thirds of the way along a hop of ×8 is 4.
Now change the numbers and run it again. Try `small_hop(27, 3) ** 2`,
or `small_hop(16, 4) ** 3`.

Here is a check of the two paths. `==` asks "is this equal to that?"
Python answers `True` or `False`.

```python exec
id: finding-the-small-hop-by-trying-2
path_one = small_hop(8, 3) ** 2
path_two = small_hop(8 ** 2, 3)
print(path_one, path_two)
print(path_one == path_two)
```

```predict
type: choice

Before you run it: what will the last line print?

- True
- False
  - The two paths do different things in a different order. It can
    feel like they should land in different places.
```

Path 1 finds the small hop and takes two. Path 2 takes two whole hops,
and then finds a third. Both print 4, and the last line prints `True`.
Try the same check with 27 in place of 8.

## The usual way to write it

On the page *Halfway powers: fractional exponents, two half steps make
one*, halfway(9) had a usual way to write it: $9^{1/2}$. edge(8) had
one too: $8^{1/3}$. Here they are again:

$$\text{halfway}(9) = 9^{1/2} = 3 \qquad \text{edge}(8) = 8^{1/3} = 2$$

Two thirds of the way along a hop of ×8 is two small hops, edge(8) ×
edge(8). With the usual sign, that is $8^{1/3} \times 8^{1/3}$, or
$\left(8^{1/3}\right)^2$.

The page *A power of a power: stacks of stacks* multiplied the two
exponents. A third, two times, is two thirds:

$$\left(8^{1/3}\right)^2 = 8^{\frac{1}{3} \times 2} = 8^{2/3} = 4$$

Path 2 gives the same sign: $\left(8^2\right)^{1/3} = 8^{2 \times \frac{1}{3}} = 8^{2/3}$.

An exponent that is a fraction is called a *fractional exponent*. For
any number, with shapes:

$$\heartsuit^{\triangle / \square}$$

- The bottom, $\square$, says how many small hops make one whole hop.
- The top, $\triangle$, says how many small hops to take.

With letters, the same thing is $b^{m/n}$. Here are the numbers from
this page, the usual way:

$$8^{2/3} = 4 \qquad 27^{2/3} = 9 \qquad 9^{3/2} = 27 \qquad 16^{3/4} = 8$$

In Python, $8^{2/3}$ is `8 ** (2/3)`. The brackets make Python find
2/3 first.

```python exec
id: the-usual-way-to-write-it-1
print(8 ** (2/3))
print(27 ** (2/3))
print(9 ** (3/2))
print(16 ** (3/4))
```

The last two lines print `27.0` and `8.0`. The first two print
`3.9999999999999996` and `8.999999999999998`. Python writes 2/3 as a
decimal, and it stops after about 16 digits. So the answer is a tiny bit
different from 4. This is why the page found small hops by trying
whole numbers. Whole numbers do not round.

You can keep saying "two thirds of the way along a hop of ×8" when it
feels calmer. It means exactly $8^{2/3}$.

## Your rule, in your words

Before you read our version, say what the top and the bottom of the
fraction do, in your own words. Write it in the Notes panel or on
paper, or say it aloud.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way to say it</summary>

The bottom of the fraction cuts one whole hop into equal small hops.
The top says how many small hops to take. So $8^{2/3}$ is edge(8),
taken twice: 2 × 2 = 4. We can also take the whole hops first and cut
them afterwards: edge(8 × 8) is 4 too. Your way of saying it may be
clearer than ours.

</details>
