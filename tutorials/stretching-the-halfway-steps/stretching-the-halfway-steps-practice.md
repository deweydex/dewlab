# Stretching the halfway steps: fractional exponents, the top and the bottom — Practice

Small problems on one idea. A hop is cut into equal small hops. The
bottom of the fraction says how many small hops make one whole hop. The
top says how many small hops to take. Try each problem before you open
anything under it. Choose numbers, shapes or letters in the box under
the title.

<img src="thirds-of-eight.svg" alt="A number line with stops at 1, 2, 4 and 8. A big arc labelled ×8 hops from 1 to 8. Under it, three small arcs labelled ×2 hop from 1 to 2, from 2 to 4 and from 4 to 8. Under the stops are the fractions 0, 1/3, 2/3 and 1, with the words: how far along, in whole hops.">

## 1. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Here are three numbers. The first is two thirds of the way along a hop
of ×8. The second is edge(64). The third is two thirds of 8. Which two
are the same?

- The first and the second
  - Two small hops of ×2 land on 4. And 4 × 4 × 4 is 64, so edge(64)
    is 4 too.
- The first and the third
  - Two thirds of 8 is 5 and a third. Two small hops of ×2 land on 4.
- The second and the third
  - edge(64) is 4, because 4 × 4 × 4 is 64. Two thirds of 8 is 5 and
    a third.
```

## 5. Two paths, one answer

Here are two paths to two thirds of the way along a hop.

- **Path 1.** Find the small hop first. Then take two of them.
- **Path 2.** Take two whole hops first. Then find a third of them.

Python can check the numbers. This is the small hop finder from the
page. It tries whole numbers until one, done `parts` times, reaches
the big hop. In Python, `**` means a power.

```python exec
id: two-paths-one-answer-2
def small_hop(big_hop, parts):
    guess = 1
    while guess ** parts < big_hop:
        guess = guess + 1
    return guess

print(small_hop(27, 3) ** 2)
print(small_hop(27 * 27, 3))
```

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Path 1: 3 × 3 × 3 is 27, so edge(27) is 3. Two small hops land on
3 × 3, which is 9.

Path 2: 27 × 27 is 729. And 9 × 9 × 9 is 729, so edge(729) is 9.

The two paths land in the same place, 9. The cell prints 9 twice.

</details>

## 6. Closer to 2, to 5 or to 7?

No whole number lands on this one. Make a guess first.

```question

type: multiple-choice
answer: 2

Two thirds of the way along a hop of ×10. Is it closer to 2, to 5 or
to 7?

- Closer to 2
  - One small hop is a bit more than 2. This asks about two small hops.
- Closer to 5
  - One small hop is a bit more than 2, so two of them are a bit more

- Closer to 7
  - Two thirds of 10 is 6 and two thirds. But hops multiply.

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

   3.
2. 10 is much closer to 8 than to 27. So edge(10) is a bit more than 2.
3. Two small hops multiply by edge(10) twice.

**Think about:** what is 2 × 2? And what is 3 × 3?

**Try this next:** is one third of the way along a hop of ×100 closer
to 3, to 5 or to 33?

</details>

```

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The first line prints `9.938374999999999`, which is close to 10. So

thirds of the way along a hop of ×10 is about 4.6. That is closer to 5.

## 7. What went differently here?

Here is a worked problem, with one step that went differently.

> Two thirds of the way along a hop of ×27. 27 ÷ 3 is 9, and 9 × 2 is
> 18. So it is 18.

What went differently? Check it by hopping: where do small hops of ×3
land, starting at 1?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Write the stops: 1, 3, 9, 27.
2. Is 18 one of the stops?
3. Count two small hops from 1.

**Think about:** "27 ÷ 3 × 2" finds two thirds of 27 as a distance.
Does a hop add, or multiply?

**Try this next:** two thirds of the way along a hop of ×125, by
hopping.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The worked problem found two thirds of 27 by dividing and multiplying.
That treats the hop as a distance to share out. But each small hop
multiplies. edge(27) is 3, and two small hops land on 3 × 3, which is
9. 18 is not a stop on the line.

</details>

## 9. Match three ways

```question
id: match-three-ways-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

A hop of ×16 is cut into 4 small hops, and we take 3 of them. Which of
these says the same thing?

- $16^{3/4}$, which is 8
  - The bottom, 4, cuts the hop into 4. The top, 3, takes 3 of them:
    2 × 2 × 2 is 8.
- $16^{4/3}$
  - The top and the bottom have changed places. That cuts the hop into
    3, and takes 4.
- $16 \times \frac{3}{4}$, which is 12
  - That is three quarters of 16, as a distance. But hops multiply.
```

## 10. A stretch: five halves

The top can be bigger than the bottom. Then we go past one whole hop.

```question
id: a-stretch-five-halves-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

A hop of ×4 is cut into halves. Each small hop is halfway(4), which is
{2|8|1}.

Five half hops, starting at 1, land on
{32|10|20}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. 2 × 2 is 4, so each half hop multiplies by 2.
2. Start at 1. Double it five times, and write each number down.
3. Where do you land?

**Think about:** five halves is two whole hops and one half hop more.
Two whole hops of ×4 land on 16.

**Try this next:** what might zero halves of the way along be? Where
do zero small hops leave you?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

halfway(4) is 2. Five half hops: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. So five halves of the
way along hops of ×4 lands on 32. Another path: two whole hops of ×4
land on 16, and one half hop more doubles it to 32.

</details>

## 12. From earlier: another name for a half

From *Equivalent fractions: the same amount, different names*.
$\frac{2}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ are the same amount. A hop of ×16 is
cut into 4 small hops. Where is two quarters of the way along? Is it
the same as halfway(16)?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 is 16. So each quarter hop multiplies by 2.
2. Take two quarter hops from 1.
3. Now find halfway(16). Which number, times itself, gives 16?

**Think about:** two slices of a pizza cut into 4 are half of the
pizza.

**Try this next:** is three sixths of the way along a hop of ×64 the
same as halfway(64)?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

Two quarter hops of ×2 land on 4. halfway(16) is 4 too, because
4 × 4 is 16. So two quarters of the way along is halfway along, as the
pizza says.

</details>

## 13. From earlier: zero of the way along

From *The zero power: when everything cancels*. $\heartsuit^0$ is 1.
Now think of a hop cut into thirds. What is zero thirds of the way
along? Where do zero small hops leave you, starting at 1?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

Zero small hops leave you where you started, at 1. So zero thirds of
the way along is 1. The picture of the thirds of 8 shows it: 1 has 0
under it. That agrees with $\heartsuit^0 = 1$.

</details>
