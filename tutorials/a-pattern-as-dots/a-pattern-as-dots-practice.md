# Graphs: a pattern as dots, a line or a curve — Practice

Small problems on one idea. Draw a dot for each step of a pattern: the
step number across, the value up. Adding the same amount at each step
makes a straight line. Multiplying by the same amount makes a curve.
Try each problem before you open anything under it. Choose numbers,
shapes or letters in the box under the title.

<img src="two-patterns.svg" alt="Two plots side by side. Each has the numbers 1 to 5 along the bottom, and a scale from 0 to 32 going up the left side. ## 1. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Here are three patterns: 3, 6, 9, 12 and 1, 4, 7, 10 and 1, 3, 9, 27.
Which two make a straight line?

- 3, 6, 9, 12 and 1, 4, 7, 10
  - Both have the gaps 3, 3, 3. They start in different places, but
    they climb the same way.
- 3, 6, 9, 12 and 1, 3, 9, 27
  - Both have a 3 and a 9 in them. Look at the gaps of 1, 3, 9, 27:
    2, 6 and 18.
- 1, 4, 7, 10 and 1, 3, 9, 27
  - Both start at 1. Look at the gaps after that.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Take one pattern. Find the gaps between the numbers next to each
   other.
2. Are the gaps all the same?
3. Do the same for the other two patterns.

**Think about:** a straight line needs equal gaps. Where the pattern
starts does not matter.

**Try this next:** does 2, 5, 8, 11 make a straight line?

</details>

## 3. Fill the gap

This pattern multiplies by the same amount at each step.

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">


</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

```question

♡, ♡ × △, ?, ♡ × △ × △ × △. The missing value is
{♡ × △ × △|♡ × △ + △|♡ + △ + △}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

```question
id: fill-the-gap-1--letters

a, ar, ?, ar³. The missing value is
{ar²|2ar|ar + r}.
```

</div>

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Look at the first two values. What were they multiplied by?
2. Multiply the second value by the same amount.
3. Check: multiply your answer once more. Do you land on the fourth
   value?

**Think about:** in a multiplying pattern, the gaps are not the same.
The multiplying is.

**Try this next:** 5, 10, ?, 40.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

3 × 2 is 6, so each step multiplies by 2. 6 × 2 is 12. Check: 12 × 2
is 24, and 24 × 2 is 48. The missing value is 12. With shapes, each
step puts on one more × △, so the missing value is ♡ × △ × △.

</details>

```question
Which pattern and which rule match this graph?

- 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and "add 5 at each step"
  - The gaps are 5, 5, 5, 5, so each dot climbs 5.
- 5, 25, 125, 625, 3125, and "multiply by 5 at each step"
  - The gaps here are 20, 100, 500 and 2500. Each dot climbs more
```

## 7. What went differently here?

## 8. Two paths, one answer

The pattern is 4, 8, 12, 16. What is its value at step 10?

- First path: keep adding 4, and count the steps to step 10.
- Second path: at each step, the value is 4 times the step number.

```question
id: two-paths-one-answer-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

The first path lands on
{40|44|36}.

The second path gives 4 × 10, which is
{40|14|410}.
```

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40. The tenth value is 40. And
4 × 10 is 40 too. For the doubling pattern 2, 4, 8, 16, the second
path is a power: the value at step 10 is $2^{10} = 1024$.

</details>

## 10. Closer to 0, to a half, or to 1?

This pattern climbs towards 1: 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, 15/16. Its gaps are 1/4,
1/8 and 1/16, so each gap is half the last one.

```question
id: closer-to-0-a-half-or-1-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

15/16 is closest to
{1|a half|0}.

The gaps get smaller, so the dots make
{a curve|a straight line}.

1/16 is closest to
{0|a half|1}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. Think of a pizza cut into 16 slices. 15/16 is every slice except one.
2. 1/16 is one of those thin slices.
3. For the shape, look at the gaps. Are they the same, or do they
   get smaller?

**Think about:** 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, 15/16. How much is missing from 1 each
time?

**Try this next:** what is the next value after 15/16?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

15/16 is 1 − 1/16, so it is very close to 1. The gaps shrink, so the
dots make a curve that flattens as it comes near 1. It never quite
reaches 1, in the same way the halving pattern never reaches 0. 1/16 is
very close to 0.

</details>

## 11. A stretch: square numbers

The bead squares from *Undoing a square: square roots, the side of a
square* hold 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25 beads. Is this pattern adding or
multiplying? Find its gaps. Then find the gaps between the gaps.

The cell draws its graph. Change the values to try other patterns.

```python exec
id: a-stretch-square-numbers-1
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

steps = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]
values = [1, 4, 9, 16, 25]

plt.plot(steps, values, "o")
```

The first line, `import`, gets Python's drawing tools ready.

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The gaps are 4 − 1, 9 − 4, 16 − 9 and 25 − 16.
2. Are they the same? Then it adds the same amount.
3. Now divide: 4 ÷ 1, then 9 ÷ 4. Is it the same multiplying each time?

**Think about:** a pattern can grow without adding the same amount,
and without multiplying by the same amount.

**Try this next:** what are the next two square numbers? Do their gaps
follow the pattern?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The gaps are 3, 5, 7 and 9. They grow, so the dots make a curve. But
the pattern does not multiply by the same amount: 4 ÷ 1 is 4, and 9 ÷ 4
is 9/4. It is neither kind from the page. The gaps between the gaps
are 2, 2 and 2. So the gaps themselves make a straight line.

</details>

## 12. From earlier: powers, the long way

From *Powers: the long way and the short way*. The pattern 3, 9, 27, 81
is $3^1, 3^2, 3^3, 3^4$. What is the value at step 5? Write it the long
way, then as a number.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

$3^5 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 243$. Its dots make a
curve, because each step multiplies by 3.

</details>

## 13. From earlier: counting hops

From *Counting hops: logarithms, how many times did we multiply?*. In
the pattern 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, which step has the value 32? What is
hops(2 → 32)?

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>answer</summary>

Step 5 has the value 32, and hops(2 → 32) is 5. The step number counts
the hops of ×2. So on the doubling graph, hops tells you how far across
a value is.

</details>
