# Mixed problems: square roots and logarithms

These problems use all the pages on square roots and logarithms. They
are mixed on purpose. A problem does not say which page it comes from.
It does not say which friendly name it needs, either. Choosing is part
of the problem.

Every friendly name here undoes a power. Each one asks one of two
questions:

- **Which number was multiplied?** side, edge and halfway ask this.
- **How many times did we multiply?** hops and folds ask this.

So before each problem, ask: which question is this? Which number was
multiplied, or how many times?

The usual signs sometimes sit beside a friendly name: √ for side, a
fraction in the exponent for halfway and edge, and log for hops. A sign
means exactly the same as its friendly name. Use whichever feels
calmer.

Try each problem before you open anything under it. There is no hurry,
and no score. Under each problem, "stuck? here are some steps" says
which page the idea comes from. Some problems come with numbers, shapes
or letters. Choose the way you like in the box under the title.

The counting machine from the hops pages can check the numbers. It
starts at 1, multiplies by the base, and counts until it reaches the
target. In Python, `**` means a power.

```python exec
id: hops-meet-powers-2
def hops(base, target):
    position = 1
    count = 0
    while position < target:
        position = position * base
        count = count + 1
    return count

print(hops(2, 2 ** 5 * 2 ** 3))
print(hops(2, (2 ** 5) ** 3))
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Counting hops: logarithms, how many times
   did we multiply?*, *Hops that add: logarithms of a product, and the
   slide rule* and *The rules of powers: everything at once*.
2. hops of a power is its exponent. hops(2 → 2⁵) counts five hops of
   ×2.
3. Make the inside one power first: join two stacks, count boxes, or
   cross out pairs. Then read its exponent.

**Think about:** 2⁵ × 2³ is five hops of ×2, and then three more.

**Try this next:** hops(10 → (10²)³ × 10).

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

- $2^5$ is five hops of ×2, so hops(2 → $2^5$) is 5.
- $2^5 \times 2^3 = 2^8$: five hops, then three more. So it is 8.
- $\left(2^5\right)^3 = 2^{15}$: three boxes of five hops. So it is 15.
- $2^5 / 2^3 = 2^2$: five hops, then three back. So it is 2.

The cell prints 8 and 15. The same counts work for any base.

</details>

```question
{7|3.5|49}.

edge(10) × edge(10) × edge(10) is exactly
{10|30|1000}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Hops that add: logarithms of a product,
   and the slide rule*, *Sides that never end: surds, the square root
   of 2* and *Halfway powers: fractional exponents, two half steps make
   one*.
2. Hops of a product add. So multiply the two numbers first: 2 × 500.
3. side(8) is 2 × side(2). And side(2) × side(2) is 2.

**Think about:** two halfway hops make one whole hop. Three edge hops
make one whole hop too.

**Try this next:** hops(10 → 4) + hops(10 → 250).

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

- 2 × 500 is 1000, and hops(10 → 1000) is 3.
- side(2) × side(8) is side(2) × 2 × side(2). That is 2 × 2, which is
  4. It is side(16) too.
- Two halfway(7)s make 7.
- Three edge(10)s make 10.

</details>

## 13. Continue the pattern

Here is a row of numbers. Each one is the number before it, times
itself.

$$2 \qquad 4 \qquad 16 \qquad 256$$

folds(2) is 1. folds(4) is 2. folds(16) is 4.

```question
id: continue-the-pattern-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

folds(256) is
{8|16|128}.

The next number is 256 × 256, which is 65,536. folds(65,536) is
{16|9|32,768}.

Now undo one step. side(256) is
{16|128|8}.

So taking the side
{halves the folds|doubles the folds|takes 1 from the folds}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Counting hops: logarithms, how many times
   did we multiply?*, *Halfway powers: fractional exponents, two half
   steps make one* and *A power of a power: stacks of stacks*.
2. Write each number as a power of 2: $2^1$, $2^2$, $2^4$, $2^8$.
   folds of a power of 2 is its exponent.
3. A number times itself is two boxes of the same stack. So the
   exponent doubles.

**Think about:** side undoes "times itself". What does it do to the
exponent?

**Try this next:** what comes after 65,536 in the row? What are its
folds?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

256 is $2^8$, so folds(256) is 8. 65,536 is $\left(2^8\right)^2 = 2^{16}$,
so its folds are 16. side(256) is 16, because 16 × 16 is 256. And
folds(16) is 4, half of 8.

Squaring doubles the folds, and taking the side halves them. The next
number in the row is 65,536 × 65,536, which is 4,294,967,296. That is
$2^{32}$, with 32 folds.

</details>

## 14. Match three ways

```question
id: match-three-ways-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 1

Which words, friendly name and sign go together?

- A cube of 1000 beads has 10 beads along each edge, and
  edge(1000) = 10, and $1000^{1/3} = 10$
  - 10 × 10 × 10 is 1000, and a third in the exponent is edge.
- Three hops of ×10 go from 1 to 1000, and hops(10 → 1000) = 3, and
  $\sqrt{1000} = 3$
  - The words and the friendly name fit. But √ is the sign for side,
    and 3 × 3 is 9.
- A square of 1000 beads has 10 beads along each side, and
  side(1000) = 10, and $\sqrt{1000} = 10$
  - 10 × 10 is only 100. A square of 1000 beads has between 31 and 32
    along each side.
- Ten folds make 1000 pieces, and folds(1000) = 10, and
  $\log_2 1000 = 10$
  - Ten folds make 1024 pieces, a little more than 1000.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses "The usual way to write it" from *Undoing a square: square
   roots, the side of a square*, *Halfway powers: fractional exponents,
   two half steps make one* and *Counting hops: logarithms, how many
   times did we multiply?*.
2. For each choice, check the words with a small sum.
3. Then check that the sign says the same as the friendly name.

**Think about:** in three of the choices, two parts fit and one does
not. Which part is it each time?

**Try this next:** write the words and the sign for folds(1024) = 10.

</details>

## 15. Paper, in 64 pieces

An A0 sheet is cut in half. Then each half is cut in half, and so on,
until there are 64 equal sheets. Each cut makes the next size: A1, A2,
A3, …

```question
id: paper-in-64-pieces-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

folds(64) is
{6|8|32}.

So the 64 sheets are the size
{A6|A8|A32}.

Each small sheet has the same shape as A0. Its long side is about
{1.414|2|0.707}
times its short side.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Surds and logs in the wild: A4 paper and
   the largest prime* and *Counting hops: logarithms, how many times
   did we multiply?*.
2. Each cut doubles the number of sheets: 1, 2, 4, 8, …
3. Count the cuts from 1 sheet to 64. Each cut is one step in the size
   name.

**Think about:** one A0 sheet makes 16 A4 sheets, and folds(16) is 4.

**Try this next:** how many A10 sheets does one A0 sheet make?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

folds(64) is 6, so the sheets are A6. Every A sheet keeps the same
shape, with a long side of side(2) short sides. That is $\sqrt{2}$,
about 1.414. A6 is 105 mm by 148 mm, and 148 ÷ 105 is about 1.41.

</details>

## 16. How many digits?

$8^{10}$ is a big number. How many digits does it have? Can you find it
with hops, without writing the number out?

```question
id: how-many-digits-1
type: fill-in-the-blank

8 is 2³. So 8¹⁰ is 2 to the power
{30|13|80}.

Each hop of ×2 is about 0.30103 hops of ×10. So 8¹⁰ is about
30 × 0.30103 = 9.0309 hops of ×10. It has
{10|9|30}
digits.
```

Python can check. `str` turns a number into text, and `len` counts the
characters in the text.

```python exec
id: how-many-digits-2
print(8 ** 10)
print(len(str(8 ** 10)))
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *A power of a power: stacks of stacks* and
   *Surds and logs in the wild: A4 paper and the largest prime*.
2. $8^{10}$ is 10 boxes, with three 2s in each box. Multiply the
   exponents.
3. The digits are the whole part of the hops, plus one.

**Think about:** $2^{10}$ is 1024, close to 1000. That is about three
hops of ×10.

**Try this next:** how many digits does $4^{50}$ have?

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$8^{10} = \left(2^3\right)^{10} = 2^{30}$. That is about 9.0309 hops of
×10. The whole part is 9, so it has 9 + 1 = 10 digits. The cell prints
1073741824, and then 10.

</details>

## 17. A stretch: part of a hop

A hop can be cut into equal small hops. Then hops can count a part of a
hop.

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">

```question
id: a-stretch-part-of-a-hop-1--numbers
type: fill-in-the-blank

halfway(4) is 2. So hops(4 → 2) is
{1/2|2|4}.

edge(8) is 2. So hops(8 → 2) is
{1/3|3|4}.

Two small hops of ×2 land on 4. So hops(8 → 4) is
{2/3|3/2|2}.

27 is three half hops of ×9. So hops(9 → 27) is
{3/2|3|1/3}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

```question
id: a-stretch-part-of-a-hop-1--squiggles
type: fill-in-the-blank

hops(♡ → halfway(♡)) is
{1/2|2|♡}.

hops(♡ → edge(♡)) is
{1/3|3|♡}.

hops(♡⁶ → ♡⁴) is
{2/3|3/2|2}.

hops(♡² → ♡³) is
{3/2|2/3|1}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

```question
id: a-stretch-part-of-a-hop-1--letters
type: fill-in-the-blank

hops(b → halfway(b)) is
{1/2|2|b}.

hops(b → edge(b)) is
{1/3|3|b}.

hops(b⁶ → b⁴) is
{2/3|3/2|2}.

hops(b² → b³) is
{3/2|2/3|1}.
```

</div>

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. This uses the ideas from *Stretching the halfway steps: fractional
   exponents, the top and the bottom*, *Halfway powers: fractional
   exponents, two half steps make one* and *Counting hops: logarithms,
   how many times did we multiply?*.
2. One whole hop of ×4 goes from 1 to 4. Two equal small hops make it.
3. halfway(4) is where the first small hop lands. How much of a whole
   hop is that?

**Think about:** the bottom of the fraction counts the small hops in a
whole hop. The top counts how many we take.

**Try this next:** hops(16 → 8).

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

- 2 is one of two small hops of ×4, so hops(4 → 2) is 1/2.
- 2 is one of three small hops of ×8, so hops(8 → 2) is 1/3.
- 4 is two of three small hops of ×8, so hops(8 → 4) is 2/3.
- 27 is three half hops of ×9, so hops(9 → 27) is 3/2.

With shapes, ♡⁴ is two of three small hops of ×♡⁶, because each small
hop is ♡². With the usual signs, the first one is $\log_4 2 = 1/2$.

</details>
