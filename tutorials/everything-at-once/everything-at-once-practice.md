# The rules of powers: everything at once — Practice

Small problems that each need two or three rules. Make the top one
power, and the bottom one power. Then divide. When you forget a rule,
write it the long way, or check with a small number. Try each problem
before you open anything under it. Choose numbers, shapes or letters in
the box under the title.

## 3. Two paths, one answer

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

```question
id: two-paths-1--squiggles
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path, the inside first. ♡⁶/♡² is
{♡⁴|♡³|♡⁸}.

Then (♡⁴)³ is
{♡¹²|♡⁷|♡⁶⁴}.

Second path. (♡⁶/♡²)³ is three copies of ♡⁶/♡², multiplied. The tops
join into ♡¹⁸, and the bottoms join into
{♡⁶|♡⁵|♡⁸}.

Then ♡¹⁸/♡⁶ is
{♡¹²|♡³|♡²⁴}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

```question

First path, the inside first. w⁶/w² is
{w⁴|w³|w⁸}.

Then (w⁴)³ is
{w¹²|w⁷|w⁶⁴}.

Second path. (w⁶/w²)³ is three copies of w⁶/w², multiplied. The tops
join into w¹⁸, and the bottoms join into
{w⁶|w⁵|w⁸}.

Then w¹⁸/w⁶ is
{w¹²|w³|w²⁴}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">

```question
id: two-paths-1--numbers
type: fill-in-the-blank

First path, the inside first. 2⁶/2² is
{2⁴|2³|2⁸}.

Then (2⁴)³ is
{2¹²|2⁷|2⁶⁴}.

Second path. (2⁶/2²)³ is three copies of 2⁶/2², multiplied. The tops
join into 2¹⁸, and the bottoms join into
{2⁶|2⁵|2⁸}.

Then 2¹⁸/2⁶ is
{2¹²|2³|2²⁴}.
```

</div>

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. For the first path, subtract inside the brackets: 6 − 2.
2. For the second path, write the fraction three times, with × between.
3. When fractions are multiplied, the tops multiply together, and the
   bottoms multiply together.

**Think about:** why the two paths must meet.

**Try this next:** (♡⁵/♡³)⁴, in both ways.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

Both paths give ♡¹². With numbers, 2⁶/2² is 64/4 = 16, and 16³ is
4096. 2¹² is 4096 too.

</details>

## 4. Fill the gap

<div class="dl-world" data-world="squiggles">

```question

(♡^?)³ × ♡² = ♡¹⁴. The gap is
{4|12|9}.
```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="letters">

```question
id: fill-the-gap-1--letters
type: fill-in-the-blank

(r^?)³ × r² = r¹⁴. The gap is

```

</div>

<div class="dl-world" data-world="numbers">

```question
id: fill-the-gap-1--numbers
type: fill-in-the-blank

(3^?)³ × 3² = 3¹⁴. The gap is
{4|12|9}.
```

</div>

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The ♡² on the end brings 2 hearts. How many more are needed to make
   14?
2. Those hearts come from 3 boxes.
3. How many hearts go in each box?

**Think about:** working backwards, one rule at a time.

**Try this next:** (♡^?)² / ♡³ = ♡⁷.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

14 − 2 = 12, so the boxes must hold 12 hearts in total. There are 3
boxes, and 12 ÷ 3 = 4. So the gap is 4: (♡⁴)³ × ♡² = ♡¹² × ♡² = ♡¹⁴.

</details>

## 5. Same or different?

```question
id: same-or-different-1
type: multiple-choice
answer: 4

Three of these are the same power. Which one is different?

- (♡²)³ × ♡
  - 3 boxes of 2 is ♡⁶. One more heart makes ♡⁷.
- ♡⁴ × ♡³
  - Two stacks joined: 4 + 3 = 7.
- ♡¹⁴/♡⁷
```

2. Write the four exponents in a list.
3. Which exponent is not like the others?

**Think about:** 14/7 is 2, but ♡¹⁴/♡⁷ is not ♡².

## 8. What went differently here?

Somebody found (♡³)² × ♡⁴ like this. "The exponents are 3, 2 and 4.
3 + 2 + 4 = 9. So it is ♡⁹." Which step changed the amount? What is
(♡³)² × ♡⁴?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. What is (♡³)² on its own? Draw the boxes.
2. Now join that stack with ♡⁴.
3. Compare your answer with ♡⁹.

**Think about:** two different rules are in this line. Which rule
belongs to which part?

**Try this next:** check both answers with 2 in place of the heart.
(2³)² × 2⁴ is 64 × 16.

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

(♡³)² is 2 boxes of 3 hearts, so it is ♡⁶. The 3 and the 2 multiply;
they do not add. Then ♡⁶ × ♡⁴ joins two stacks: 6 + 4 = 10. So it is
♡¹⁰. With 2 in place of the heart, 64 × 16 = 1024, which is 2¹⁰. 2⁹ is
512. Adding all three exponents treats the boxes as a stack. Here is
one answer. Yours may be different and work too.

</details>

## 9. Closer to 0, a half, or 1?

```question
{closer to 0|a half|1}.

{1|a half|closer to 0}.
```

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

2. Subtract the exponents.
3. A power of −1 is one over the base: 2⁻¹ is 1/2.

**Think about:** 1/10 is a tenth. Is that nearer to 0 or to a half?

**Try this next:** (10²)²/(10³)². Closer to 0, a half, or 1?

- 2⁷/2⁸ = 2⁻¹ = 1/2.
- 10⁶/10⁷ = 10⁻¹ = 1/10. That is closer to 0.
- 5⁴/5⁴ = 5⁰ = 1.

</details>

## 10. The checking machine

Here are three lines for Python. Before you run it: which line will
print something that is not 1?


<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

2. Subtract the exponents.
3. Is the exponent 0, or something else?

**Think about:** Python writes a `/` answer with a decimal point, so 1

**Try this next:** add a line of your own that prints 100.0.

is 1, and it prints 1.0. The last line is 2¹⁰/2¹², which is 2⁻². That
is 1/4, and Python prints 0.25.

</details>

## 11. Two kinds of tile

This one is a stretch.

$$\frac{\left(\heartsuit^2 \triangle^3\right)^3}{\heartsuit^5 \triangle^9}$$

What is it, as short as you can write it? Can you check it with 2 for
the heart and 3 for the triangle?

<details class="dl-hint"><summary>stuck? here are some steps</summary>

1. The exponent outside the brackets reaches every tile inside.
2. Find the hearts on the top, and the triangles on the top.
3. Hearts cross out with hearts, and triangles with triangles.

**Think about:** each kind of tile has its own pairs.

**Try this next:** (♡△)⁵/(♡⁵△⁴).

</details>

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

The top is ♡⁶△⁹. The hearts leave ♡⁶/♡⁵ = ♡. The triangles leave
△⁹/△⁹ = 1. So it is ♡. With numbers, (2² × 3³)³/(2⁵ × 3⁹) is 2. You
can check it in the cell above: add the line
`print((2 ** 2 * 3 ** 3) ** 3 / (2 ** 5 * 3 ** 9))`. It prints 2.0.

</details>

## 13. From earlier: a half of a half of a half

From *Multiplying fractions: a fraction of a fraction*. What is
$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3$? Write it the long way first.

<details class="dl-answer"><summary>one way through it</summary>

$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times
\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{8}$. Multiply the tops, and multiply the
bottoms. It is also $2^{-3}$, from *Negative powers: more on the
bottom*: one over $2^3$.

</details>
