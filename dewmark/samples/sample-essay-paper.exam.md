title: What the essay marks reward

  The essay is marked on three things, and the marking screen shows
  the same three to your examiner.

  - **Argument and structure** (15 marks). One position, stated
    early, with each paragraph moving it forward.
  - **Evidence and examples** (15 marks). Specific cases, named and
    used, rather than mentioned.
  - **Clarity and control of language** (10 marks). Sentences that
    say one thing each, in words you control.

  A shorter essay that argues one thing well beats a longer essay
  that gestures at three.

## Section A — Reading a short argument

Read the passage below, then answer the three questions. Section A
carries 30 marks.

> **Should every lecture be recorded?**
>
> Colleges now have the tools to record every lecture, and some
> students say every lecture should be. The case sounds simple. A
> student who misses a class through illness or work can catch up. A
> student whose first language is not English can listen twice. A
> student revising in May can return to what was said in October.
>
> The case against is quieter but real. Lecturers speak differently
> to a camera than to a room: they take fewer risks, tell fewer
> stories, and stop thinking aloud. Attendance usually falls when
> recordings exist, and the students who stop coming are often the
> ones who most need the room, the routine, and the company.
> A recording preserves the words of a class but not the class.
>
> The choice, then, is not between convenience and tradition. It is
> between two different kinds of loss, and each college must decide
> which loss it can better afford.

### Question A1 — The writer's position

```answer
name: a1.position
type: short-written-answer
marks: 5
prompt: >
  In one or two sentences of your own, state the conclusion the
  writer reaches. Take care: it is not "recordings are bad".
model_answer: |
  The writer concludes that recording has real costs as well as real
  benefits, so each college must weigh the two losses and decide for
  itself. The writer does not come down on either side.
```

```marking
marks: 5
guidance:
  - 3 marks for seeing that the writer takes neither side
  - 2 marks for the idea of weighing two kinds of loss
  - a summary of only the benefits, or only the costs, earns at most 2
```

### Question A2 — How the argument is built

```question
name: a2
marks: 10
topic: analysis
```

```answer
name: a2.technique
type: long-written-answer
marks: 10
prompt: >
  Identify two techniques the writer uses to persuade, and quote the
  words where each appears. For each technique, explain what it does
  to the reader.
model_answer: |
  The writer uses the rule of three: "the room, the routine, and the
  company" and the October/May contrast pile up concrete details, so
  the loss feels real rather than claimed. The writer also uses a
  balanced turn: "not between convenience and tradition... between
  two different kinds of loss" reframes the whole question, which
  makes the writer sound fair and makes the reader trust the
  conclusion. Other defensible answers include the short blunt
  sentence "A recording preserves the words of a class but not the
  class", which lands the central distinction.
```

```marking
marks: 10
guidance:
  - 5 marks per technique - 2 for naming it, 1 for the quotation, 2
    for the effect on the reader
  - any two defensible techniques earn the marks; the model lists
    three the passage supports
```

### Question A3 — Your own view, briefly

```question
name: a3
marks: 15

```

```answer
name: a3.response
type: long-written-answer
marks: 15
prompt: >
  In about 150 words, agree or disagree with the writer's claim that
  "the students who stop coming are often the ones who most need the
  room". Give one reason from your own experience or observation, and
  one piece of evidence the writer would need to show to convince
  you fully.
model_answer: |
  A strong answer takes a clear side in the first sentence, supports
  it with one specific observed case, and then names checkable
  evidence, such as attendance figures compared with results for the
  same students. The side taken does not matter; the marks follow
  the reasoning.
```

```marking
marks: 15
guidance:
  - 5 marks for a clear stated position
  - 5 marks for one specific reason from experience or observation
  - 5 marks for naming evidence that could actually be gathered
```

### Question C1 — Writing to be acted on

```question
name: c1
marks: 30
topic: formal writing
```

The heating in your college's library has been broken for three
weeks. Students are studying in coats, and evening classes have
moved to the canteen. Write a formal letter to the college's
facilities manager. Your letter must say what is wrong and for how
long, describe the effect on students, ask for a specific action by
a specific date, and stay polite throughout.

```answer
name: c1.letter
type: essay
marks: 30
guide_words: 300
planning_box: no
```

```marking
criteria:
  - name: Content
    marks: 15
    bands:
      - 13 to 15 - the problem, its duration, its effects, and a
        dated, specific request are all present
      - 8 to 12 - most of the required content, or a vague request
      - 4 to 7 - the complaint without the request, or the reverse
      - 0 to 3 - little of the required content
  - name: Form and register
    marks: 10
    bands:
      - 8 to 10 - a proper formal letter, courteous and firm
      - 4 to 7 - mostly formal, with lapses into chat or into anger
      - 0 to 3 - not recognisable as a formal letter
  - name: Accuracy
    marks: 5
    bands:
      - 4 to 5 - spelling and punctuation support the register
      - 0 to 3 - errors that a facilities manager would notice
```
