# Outline — The Team Project

**Closes:** `PDP-LO12` — design, develop, release and review successive versions
of a program over time, in teams of three to five.
**Size:** not a tutorial.

## Why this is not a tutorial

The outcome asks for a team, a schedule, and more than one release. None of
those fit on a page of runnable cells, and pretending otherwise is how this
outcome ends up marked "covered" by something that does not cover it.

`everlearning`'s existing team activity is optional, ungraded, and
calculus-flavoured — it does not meet this. The gap analysis there calls it the
most significant remaining gap in either module, and it is the one item on
dewlab's list that dewlab cannot solve by writing a tutorial.

## What it needs instead

- **A brief** narrow enough that three to five students can finish it and wide
  enough to split into parts.
- **A schedule** with at least two releases and a review between them, since
  "over time" and "multiple versions" are the words the descriptor uses.
- **A review structure** — the Interlude tutorial already teaches reading
  someone else's code, and this is where that habit gets used for real.
- **A way of seeing individual contribution** inside a group mark.

## What dewlab can contribute

Not the project, but the ground it stands on:

- The Interlude's code-reading prompts are the review activity, already written.
- Tutorial 8's testing habit is what a second release should be checked against.
- A tutorial-shaped brief page could hold the specification and the schedule,
  with no cells at all — dewlab handles a prose-only tutorial perfectly well.
